## #2 · Overcoming Fear of Failure — My Business Journey to Success

**Source:** ~12:30 → 14:15 in the full talk
**Transcript (verbatim):**
> "Now I have spent 20 years building a pharmacy chain, and the business had become very large. We were very successful. But then one time, the NHS made a change to the way that they priced drugs and as a result of that, shortly afterwards, it wiped 1 million pounds off our profit and the business went into administration. And the business I built for a long time went bust.
>
> Now, you'd think that at that point that confirms the entire fear that we all have. Well, there you are. See, you failed. And you think that that was the end. But actually, it wasn't the end. Because I still had my purpose. I still had people, but actually I had experience. And from that incident, I actually ended up creating a new business. And that business is called Titan.
>
> When you go into business, believing in the possible is what we need to overcome our fears. Possibility is being able to achieve things that you never thought of. Being able to get through your darkest moments, to be able to get up even when you've been knocked out. That is possibility. And that's something that you all have in you."

## #6 · Pharmacy Dispensing Error Solved — My System Revolution

**Source:** ~07:12 → 09:20 in the full talk (the ICU story that led to Titan)
**Transcript (verbatim):**
> "I'm going to tell you a story. I had been now in business for a while, so this is going back 25 years. Picture this. I had a chain of pharmacies. I was the owner, I was a superintendent.
>
> We had an incident. One of my staff dispensed a drug in a pharmacy, and the pharmacist checked it and it went out. Except it was the wrong label on the wrong drug or the wrong packet. Anyway, it went out. The patient took it. Unfortunately, the patient ended up in ICU in hospital. They nearly died. The family made a complaint and as a result, a fitness to practice investigation was launched. And because I was superintendent of the business, I was the one hauled in front of fitness to practice. That whole investigation lasted for a year. It was the worst experience of my life.
>
> Fortunately, at the end of it, they decided to drop the case. But what that did for me was I went away and I thought, I cannot let this ever happen to me again. And from that I came up with a system. That system said, I'm going to make a new dispensing process where you take the packet and from that packet you scan the barcode, and if the barcode is correct, then we will issue the dispensing label.
>
> We implemented that process in all my pharmacies. And since that day we never made a dispensing error ever again. That same process is the process that Titan uses today. It is the reason why Titan is the most successful system."

**Title options:**
1. **The dispensing error that put a patient in ICU** ← recommended
2. The mistake that changed how UK pharmacy dispenses
3. A year in front of fitness to practice. Here's what I built.

**Why #1:** stakes are immediate (ICU), viewer needs to know what happened, but no answer given up front.

## #7 · The ONLY Business Skill You Need — Problem Solving

**Source:** ~06:19 → 07:12 in the full talk
**Transcript (verbatim):**
> "Now we think, okay, if you go and ask someone in business school, you think that to run a business you need to know about profit and loss, balance sheets, business plans, cash flow forecasts. Yawn. Don't need to know any of that stuff. You can hire people for all of that stuff. Hire an accountant.
>
> The only skill that you need in running a business is the ability to solve problems. If problem solving was a muscle in your body, you need to be bench pressing 100kg of this. If you can look at problems and not be phased, if you can look at problems and see opportunities, if you can look at problems and positively embrace them, then you have the ability to succeed in business."

## #8 · Enjoy Your Journey — Build a Business with Friends

**Source:** ~09:20 → 11:15 in the full talk
**Transcript (verbatim):**
> "We talked about sometimes going into business is about business plans and cash flow. You have to have a future, you have to have a pathway. You need to have your goals mapped out. And many times businesses spend a lot of time mapping out their future, what they need to do in the next five years. I believe that you do not need to know any of those things. Because the thing is, in business, you may never reach that destination.
>
> In business or in life generally, your destination isn't guaranteed. The only thing guaranteed is your journey. So make your journey something that you can enjoy. Like you go on any journey, you go on a journey with people whose company you enjoy. So surround yourself with people whose company you enjoy and enjoy the ride.
>
> Just to give you an illustration — in my business today, my CEO is my best friend from university who I met in freshers week. My CTO is my younger brother. My director of sales is my cousin. My chief information officer is a guy who joined me when he left university. My CFO is my accountant, who joined the day he qualified. The list goes on. These guys have been with me for 25 years. If you can create a business where work is not work, where your staff are not your staff but they're your friends, that your family and work does not feel like work — then you have created a culture which will allow you to really have the right ingredients for success."

**Title options:**
1. **My CEO is my best friend. My CTO is my brother.** ← recommended
2. I hired my best friend, my brother, and my cousin. Here's why.
3. Why I stopped writing five-year plans

**Why #1:** specific detail hooks — makes the viewer wonder how this actually works.

## #9 · Invest in Customers — Boost Sales, Not Just Products

**Source:** ~11:15 → 12:30 in the full talk
**Transcript (verbatim):**
> "The next P — product. A lot of times businesses make products, and we think that once we've made a product, we've got to go off and keep selling it. Product could be a service, it could be a weight loss service, it could be a menopause service, it could be a physical product. And we think that once we've made it, we have to go off and sell it. And there's a lot of pressure to increase sales and do that kind of stuff.
>
> In pharmacy, unfortunately, there's been too much stack it high, sell it cheap. You'll see this when you go into the profession, you'll see a lot of pharmacies competing with each other, and it's always a race to the bottom.
>
> I believe that actually what you need to do is you need to invest in what you have made and look after your current customers. Look after your patients. Look after the people who have already bought from you. Look after them and they will then become your advocates who will then sell whatever product or service it is that you are supplying. So add value. And especially as you're becoming IPs, you're going to be providing new services — do not undervalue yourselves. Do not undersell yourselves. Value yourselves, and sell yourselves at the right premium price."

**Title options:**
1. **Why pharmacies are stuck in a race to the bottom** ← recommended
2. Stop chasing sales. Do this instead.
3. Your existing customers are worth more than you think

**Why #1:** the "race to the bottom" is a phrase pharmacists recognise viscerally — instant curiosity about what the alternative is.
