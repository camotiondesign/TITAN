# Titan Content Operating Strategy

**Version:** v3  
**Date:** 21 August 2026  
**Status:** Live operating manual  
**Scope:** Titan PMR + Titanverse  
**Supersedes:** previous Titan social/content strategy documents where this document conflicts with them. Voice, caption craft, product knowledge, founder voice and factual reference files remain subordinate source material.

---

## 1. Purpose

This is the live operating strategy for Titan content. It is written for both humans and AI/sub-agents.

Its job is to govern:

- what deserves to become content;
- where content comes from;
- how Titan decides what to publish;
- how ideas should change by platform;
- how campaigns are chosen;
- how customer stories, events, native entertainment and industry intelligence are captured;
- what Titan colleagues need to provide to make the system work;
- how performance is reviewed without confusing volume for success.

This is not a fixed three-month calendar. The framework is intended to remain durable beyond Q4 2026. September-December 2026 is the first operating period under it.

---

## 2. Strategic objective

Titan content should build two primary outcomes:

1. **Brand awareness** — more of the right people repeatedly encounter Titan, choose to follow it, engage with it, reference it, create their own related content, or want to participate in the world Titan is creating.
2. **Category authority** — Titan becomes one of the most useful and credible sources for understanding where UK community pharmacy is going and how operators can thrive under changing conditions.

Commercial outcomes such as demo interest, Titanverse adoption, retention and advocacy matter, but there is not currently a reliable attribution system connecting social content to these outcomes. They should not be presented as directly measurable content KPIs until Titan creates that instrumentation.

### Desired content position

> **Titan is the company that best understands community pharmacy, where it is going, and how pharmacy owners can thrive under the conditions ahead.**

Titan should prove this repeatedly rather than rely on generic claims such as “built by pharmacists for pharmacists.”

---

## 3. Brand architecture: Titan PMR + Titanverse

Titan PMR and Titanverse should be treated as a **unified content ecosystem**, not two unrelated brands.

They do not need to sound identical or be forced into every post together. Their relationship should be clear:

- **Titan PMR:** the dispensary and operational foundation — safe, scalable dispensing, workflow, automation, efficiency and control.
- **Titanverse:** the clinical/business layer — services, prescribing, growth, management and the shift from dispensing-only operations towards a broader pharmacy business model.

The Titan PMR page currently has greater reach and authority. Titanverse needs stronger internal support, clearer proof and better source material before its social presence can consistently compete. The answer is not simply “post more Titanverse.”

Where a story naturally crosses both layers, show the journey. Where it does not, let the relevant layer lead.

# PART I — WHAT GOOD CONTENT IS

## 4. The four reasons to care

Every content idea must answer one question before production:

> **Why would a pharmacy owner or pharmacy professional care?**

Titan content has four valid reasons to earn attention.

### 4.1 CRAFT — worth watching

The execution itself creates an experience.

Examples:
- beautifully produced case-study films;
- TitanUp / Pharmacy Show montages;
- energetic event photography;
- humour;
- strong editing, pacing, sound design or visual storytelling;
- a concept that feels surprising, distinctive or unlike normal corporate pharmacy content.

Craft does not need to contain a lesson. It may create FOMO, emotion, enjoyment, spectacle or simply appreciation for the quality of the piece.

**Important:** craft amplifies a strong idea. It does not rescue a weak one.

### 4.2 VALUE — worth knowing

The viewer leaves with something useful.

Examples:
- industry news explained properly;
- an NHS / regulatory change and what it means;
- a new way of thinking about pharmacy economics;
- practical advice from an experienced operator;
- useful workflow education;
- a thought-leadership argument that genuinely changes perspective.

A person having an opinion is not automatically thought leadership. The viewer should be able to say:

> “I hadn’t thought about it like that before.”

### 4.3 CONNECTION — worth caring about

The viewer recognises themselves, their world or another human being worth investing in.

Connection includes:

**Recognition**
- “That happens in my pharmacy.”
- “I know that feeling.”
- “Every dispenser has done that.”
- relatable pharmacy humour and situations.

**Human investment**
- “I want to know what happened to this owner.”
- “That sounds brutal.”
- “I’m rooting for this person.”

The viewer does not need to literally see themselves in the protagonist. They only need a reason to care.

### 4.4 PROOF — worth believing / wanting

The content demonstrates a meaningful consequence rather than making a vague claim.

Examples:
- “How is this pharmacy finishing by 1pm?”
- “What pharmacy owner gets six weeks off?”
- “21,000 items. Empty picking column. Every single day.”
- 4,000 to 17,000 items while creating more time for services;
- a fifth branch switching because the first four already proved the model;
- a visible before/after workflow.

Proof is especially valuable because the customer demonstrates the claim instead of Titan declaring it.

---

## 5. The publishing gate

These four dimensions are a **hard pre-publish gate**.

### Minimum threshold
A post must hit **at least one dimension strongly**.

“Technically present” does not count. Weak value is still weak content. Weak proof is still weak proof.

### Strong content
Usually combines **two or more dimensions**.

### Hero / breakout opportunity
Should aim for **three or four dimensions** where natural.

### The hook sits above the four
The hook is the doorway. A valuable piece nobody understands in the first seconds/lines can still fail.

Every post should therefore pass:

> **HOOK → REASON TO CARE → PAYOFF**

If the team cannot articulate the reason to care in one sentence, the piece should not ship yet.

---

## 6. What the evidence says

The 2025-2026 performance analysis supports this model.

### LinkedIn: the reach environment changed, but our mix changed too

- LinkedIn median impressions fell from **1,188 in 2025 to 906 in 2026 to 10 Aug** (-24%).
- The breakout rate above 3,000 impressions fell from **9.4% to 4.1%**.
- Publishing volume increased materially while per-post reach declined.
- This does **not prove** that higher volume caused lower reach. It makes volume/content dilution a credible factor to test alongside external algorithm changes.

The useful diagnosis is:

> 2026 created more total activity, but fewer breakout posts relative to output. The opportunity is to increase hit-rate, not simply output.

### Consistent winners

Across the data, the strongest repeatable shapes include:

- **Named-owner case studies:** Prab 6,728 LinkedIn impressions; Sagar 5,356; Yusuf/BMP 3,807.
- **Named people + hard outcomes:** Balance Street 21,000 items / empty picking column; pharmacy-owner time/freedom outcomes.
- **Field/event multi-image:** TitanUp Day 1 7,325 impressions; Lansdales 3,993; Malpas 3,670.
- **Industry/editorial value:** GTIN 7,338; independent prescribing 5,440; ChatGPT in pharmacy 5,517.
- **Event broadcast:** Pharmacy Show keynote 4,632.
- **TikTok proof hooks:** “finish by 1pm” 14,924 views; “six weeks off” 9,627; “21,000 items” 13,427.

### Clear underperformance patterns

Examples include:

- customer go-live with weak proof (“no regrets”) — 64 LinkedIn impressions;
- cryptic Titanverse text teasers — 76 / 89 impressions;
- product screencast without a compelling human/problem frame — 290 impressions;
- event registration/countdown/admin content — repeatedly bottom-quartile.

The lesson is not “customer videos fail” or “product content fails.” The lesson is that content without a strong reason to care fails much more often.

# PART II — WHERE CONTENT COMES FROM

## 7. The five-source content engine

Titan should run five permanent content sources. These are not rigid content pillars. They are the **sources from which strong material enters the system**.

### 7.1 INTAKE — always-on real-world pharmacy signals

**Source:** Titan colleagues on site, travelling, implementing, meeting partners or encountering interesting pharmacy moments.

**Purpose:** create a continuous flow of authentic pharmacy reality without requiring a dedicated camera crew at every visit.

Titan colleagues use the existing intake form to upload:
- photos;
- video where appropriate;
- what they saw / experienced;
- useful context;
- unexpected comments or outcomes.

#### Minimum low-friction capture standard
Where practical:
- outside of pharmacy;
- inside dispensary/environment;
- pharmacist/owner portrait;
- group/team photo;
- consultation room or relevant operational setting;
- before/after visual if one exists;
- video is a bonus, but should be deliberate rather than generic.

#### Better implementation-day questions
Avoid:
- “How did implementation go?”
- “What are you most excited about?”

Prefer questions that uncover tension, change and future intent:
- What has pharmacy felt like for you recently?
- What was difficult before today?
- What was creating the most pressure?
- What do you want your pharmacy to look like over the next couple of years?
- What are you hoping this change allows you to do?
- If this is one of several sites: what have you already seen at the other branches?

The implementer’s job is not to create finished social content. It is to **capture useful ingredients**. Marketing does the triage and storytelling afterwards.

#### Required improvement
Provide short practical training to Titan colleagues so they understand what to look for and how to ask better questions without materially distracting from their core role.

---

### 7.2 EVENTS — concentrated access, authority and spectacle

**Source:** Pharmacy Show, TitanUp, partner innovation days, conferences, industry meet-ups and other significant events.

Events deserve professional capture where the opportunity justifies it.

They should produce seven types of material:

1. **Experience** — montages, crowds, arrivals, dinners, group photos, candid interactions, FOMO, community.
2. **Broadcast** — full talks, keynote edits, 30-90s thesis clips, quote assets, summaries.
3. **Industry pulse** — deliberate vox pops and recurring questions, not “are you enjoying the show?”
4. **Proof** — unexpected customer outcomes discovered on the floor.
5. **Contrarians / non-users** — objections, apprehension, why someone has not switched, what they believe Titan gets wrong.
6. **Ecosystem** — robotics/integration partners, workflows, shared-purpose explanations, known industry faces.
7. **Pipeline** — people and stories to follow up after the event.

#### Event questions should create editorial value
Examples:
- What is the biggest problem pharmacy owners are underestimating right now?
- What would you change about community pharmacy tomorrow?
- What is one thing you disagree with the industry on?
- What are you worried about heading into next year?
- Why have you not changed PMR?

#### Event photography matters
Multi-image field coverage is one of Titan’s strongest LinkedIn formats. Capture deliberately:
- wide crowd;
- close candid;
- recognisable person;
- customer + Titan colleague;
- partner interaction;
- laughter / human moment;
- dinner / breaking bread;
- stand queue;
- stage/audience;
- group photo;
- product/robot interaction.

The event should end with a **content pipeline**, not merely a hard drive of footage.

---

### 7.3 CASE STUDIES — flagship human transformation stories

**Source:** deliberate on-site shoots with selected pharmacy owners/operators.

**Cadence target:** approximately **one hero case study every 6-8 weeks**. Do not force a monthly quota. Scarcity helps the format feel important.

### Core principle

> **The pharmacy owner is the protagonist. Titan is the enabler.**

Case studies should be films about interesting pharmacies that reveal what Titan enables, not corporate product demonstrations disguised as testimonials.

#### Story first, outcomes as proof
Metrics and feature outcomes remain important, but they should support the human story rather than replace it.

Weak formula:
> Problem → Titan → 80% AI → faster → “we love Titan.”

Stronger structure:
> Person → tension → decision → transformation → proof → lessons → future.

The same strategic ingredients may repeat. The narrative formula should not.

#### Case-study selection should be topical
Do not choose “any good customer.” Choose customers relevant to the conversation Titan needs to own.

Examples:
- hub and spoke → find a genuinely strong hub-and-spoke operator;
- MDS → find an operator with meaningful MDS workflow;
- group growth → multi-site pharmacy group;
- clinical services → operator who has materially shifted time into services;
- robotics → customer where PMR/robot interoperability is central.

Maintain a **live customer-opportunity spreadsheet** containing:
- pharmacy/contact;
- why their story is unique;
- topics they can credibly speak on;
- operating model / size / services / automation;
- suitability for current campaigns.

Titan owns keeping this source list alive. Marketing can then qualify and execute.

#### Every hero shoot should create a content bank
Use the day to capture:
- hero 3-5 minute story;
- clean photography and portraits;
- group/team photos;
- proof clips;
- advice/value clips;
- platform-native entertainment concepts;
- dispenser-led workflow demos;
- before/after scenes;
- multi-image material;
- article/interview material;
- deeper YouTube version where justified.

Do not think “eight cuts from one film.” Think **multiple distinct editorial ideas from one deep conversation**.

#### Product detail in hero films
Show enough product/workflow to explain the transformation:
- scanning;
- labels/barcodes;
- Titan Mobile;
- organised flow;
- metrics screens where appropriate;
- real workflow.

Never expose patient-identifiable data.

Deep feature mechanics can live in derivative demos/articles rather than dominate the human film.

#### Craft standard
Hero case studies should feel unlike conventional corporate medical content. Distinctive openings, music, pacing, location storytelling and creative structure are encouraged.

The historical Prab work demonstrates the key rule:

> **Story strength matters more than camera specification. Production quality multiplies the story; it does not create it.**

---

### 7.4 NATIVE ENTERTAINMENT — pharmacy culture and platform-first creativity

**Primary platform:** TikTok, with selective adaptation elsewhere.

**Purpose:** build audience affinity and follower growth by making Titan a place pharmacy people genuinely want to follow — not only a place they visit when considering software.

This includes:
- POVs;
- pharmacy humour;
- relatable situations;
- trends;
- lightweight scripted scenes;
- pain → resolution concepts;
- actor/creator-led pharmacy content;
- content with no explicit Titan product mention.

A non-product entertainment post can be successful because the account itself owns the attention. A follower acquired through humour may later encounter proof, case studies and product relevance.

#### Evidence / precedent
Historical Titan native entertainment included dispenser-led content around the 20,000-view range. Recent Titan TikTok proof-led pieces also show strong native potential. Avonnex, an integration partner, is currently demonstrating that the pharmacy-tech category can communicate successfully through native creator-style material.

The opportunity is not to copy Avonnex. Titan already proved this approach can work and should rebuild a repeatable production system around it.

#### Three production routes

1. **Case-study-day bolt-on** — arrive with 5-10 simple native concepts.
2. **Dedicated pharmacy content days** — half-day/full-day production with 20-30 pre-developed concepts.
3. **Recurring pharmacy creators** — identify genuine pharmacists/dispensers/owners comfortable on camera and pay for ongoing participation.

Use a mixture where practical.

#### Proposed test budget
Consider approximately **£1,000/month** as a native-content test budget, with individual created pieces historically achievable around **£100-£200 per piece**, subject to finding suitable pharmacy creators and commercial approval.

This is a content production budget, not paid-media spend.

#### Do not force Titan into every punchline
If every entertaining post ends “and that’s why we use Titan,” the audience learns it is an advert.

Some content should simply be good pharmacy content.

---

### 7.5 EDITORIAL INTELLIGENCE — authority through interpretation

**Source:** live pharmacy news + Titan’s internal expertise.

**Purpose:** prove that Titan understands the industry by interpreting what is happening and explaining what it means for operators.

This includes:
- reactive news;
- NHS/regulatory changes;
- industry economics;
- hub and spoke;
- weight-management/service trends;
- independent prescribing;
- competitor/incumbent moves;
- AI/automation;
- cybersecurity/technology changes;
- carousel deep dives;
- expert reaction videos;
- practical education.

### Core editorial formula

> **What happened? → Why does it matter? → What should a pharmacy owner think/do differently?**

Do not merely repeat the news.

#### Low-friction expert call
Formalise:
- **one monthly expert conversation** as a baseline;
- **reactive calls whenever a genuinely worthwhile issue emerges**.

Potential voices include Hooman, Jeff, Tariq, Wahid and the best-qualified person for the topic in question.

The call can be a simple recorded Google Meet. The low production friction is a feature, not a weakness.

One conversation can create:
- LinkedIn video;
- TikTok cut;
- carousel;
- quote/static;
- article/newsletter input;
- follow-up questions.

Expert voices should also act as **drivers**: if they see an issue Titan should respond to, they should surface it to marketing.

## 8. Product demos, launches and feature releases

Product/feature content is important, but it is **not a sixth always-on content source**. It is a specialist workstream that needs stronger internal briefing than the five-source engine.

### Demo principle
Avoid long granular demonstrations where the audience has no reason to care.

Prefer:
> **Have you ever had this problem? → Look how Titan handles it → here is the outcome.**

Demos performed by real dispensers/users in a real pharmacy can be stronger than sterile studio walkthroughs.

### Product-launch dependency
For substantial launches, animated demos or product videos, Titan must provide a clear brief containing:
- what changed;
- why it matters;
- target audience/problem;
- workflow truth;
- approved claims;
- relevant screens/assets;
- priority timing;
- internal product owner;
- storyboard/input where needed.

Marketing should not be expected to discover releases through overheard conversations and independently reconstruct product strategy.

Where animation is required, internal product/marketing stakeholders should provide the structured story/brief before production begins.

# PART III — HOW TOPICS ARE CHOSEN

## 9. Campaign territories

Campaigns are a **theme layer across the five sources**, not another content source.

Robotics is the model:
- agitate the interoperability problem;
- use memes/recognition;
- explain the workflow/value;
- show partner/ecosystem proof;
- capture customer proof;
- culminate in events/innovation days/demos;
- avoid endlessly saying “Titan works with robots.”

### Campaign activation rule
A strategic campaign activates when these three intersect:

> **BUSINESS PRIORITY + LIVE INDUSTRY CONVERSATION + AUDIENCE TENSION**

If one is missing, do not force a “theme month” for calendar symmetry.

Potential territories:
- robotics / interoperability;
- hub and spoke;
- MDS;
- clinical services / Titanverse;
- independent prescribing;
- AI / automation;
- pharmacy economics / profitability;
- other live issues surfaced by Titan teams.

### Internal intelligence requirement
Titan teams must proactively surface:
- recurring objections;
- competitor perceptions;
- frequently asked questions;
- support/sales themes;
- upcoming feature priorities;
- customer success patterns;
- events/innovation days at least several weeks ahead where possible.

Marketing cannot own a conversation it does not know is happening.

# PART IV — PLATFORM STRATEGY

## 10. Primary and secondary channels

### 10.1 LinkedIn — primary

**Job:** authority, professional relevance, customer transformation, industry interpretation and serious business proof.

Prioritise:
- named-owner case studies;
- multi-image field/event posts;
- industry/news analysis;
- strong founder/expert arguments on the company page;
- useful carousels;
- longer-form 16:9 video when the story earns attention;
- event broadcast;
- proof-rich customer/operational stories.

### LinkedIn publishing principle

> **Selectivity over quota.**

Do not post because the calendar says a slot exists.

2026 showed substantially higher posting volume without a proportional improvement in per-post reach or breakout frequency. External algorithm changes are a real caveat, so this is not proof that volume itself caused the decline. It is enough evidence to stop treating volume as the goal.

Normal periods should favour fewer, stronger pieces. Event/campaign windows can increase cadence when there is genuinely more worth saying.

Personal-profile amplification is outside this strategy’s operational ownership. Titan leaders may amplify content independently, but business-channel delivery should not depend on Cam managing personal profiles.

---

### 10.2 TikTok — primary

**Job:** attention, discovery, follower growth, pharmacy culture and fast proof.

Prioritise:
- native entertainment;
- specific numerical outcomes;
- pharmacy-owner stories;
- recognised pharmacy situations;
- sector opinions with hard hooks;
- quick demos framed around problems;
- platform-native captions/open loops;
- creator/pharmacy cast content.

TikTok should not be treated as vertical LinkedIn.

A 3-minute LinkedIn case study may become a 30-second TikTok built around one outcome. Some TikTok content should never appear on LinkedIn.

### TikTok success metrics

Track:
- views;
- follower growth;
- shares;
- saves;
- comments;
- completion/watch time when available;
- breakout frequency.

Follower growth matters because the aim is to build a pharmacy audience that repeatedly chooses Titan content, not merely accumulate isolated views.

---

### 10.3 Instagram — active experimental/distribution channel

Instagram is growing but currently lacks enough reliable native evidence to define a mature standalone strategy.

For now:
- distribute strong TikTok/visual material;
- use photography, events, people and pharmacy environments;
- watch for content that consistently outperforms baseline;
- refine native execution when a repeatable signal appears.

Do not prematurely conclude Instagram cannot work simply because repurposed content underperformed.

---

### 10.4 YouTube — long-tail library and depth

YouTube is a compounding channel rather than an immediate-reach channel.

Use for:
- hero case studies;
- talks/keynotes;
- extended expert interviews;
- useful demos;
- future podcast/deep editorial work.

Judge it on its own analytics and long-tail accumulation, not on first-week TikTok/LinkedIn expectations.

---

### 10.5 Facebook — low-priority distribution

Cross-post appropriate content where low-friction. Do not spend meaningful bespoke creative time unless the data changes.

---

## 11. Cross-platform rule

Do not default to:
> “Make one asset and resize it.”

Default to:
> **Capture one valuable story or idea, then create the best expression for each relevant platform.**

Example customer story:
- LinkedIn: 3-4 minute transformation film + multi-image proof;
- TikTok: “How is this pharmacy finishing at 1pm?”;
- Instagram: human/visual Reel or photography story;
- YouTube: full film / extended version;
- website: written case study.

The source material can be shared. The editorial product should be platform-native.

# PART V — OPERATING MODEL

## 12. Content opportunity flow

### Customer stories

> **Titan builds/supplies opportunity list → Cam qualifies → Cam owns execution.**

Titan should maintain a living customer-story spreadsheet. The exact internal owner is for Titan to assign.

The key requirement is not more meetings. It is a reliable stream of names, context and permission to approach.

### General intake
Titan colleagues upload real-world material through the existing intake system. Marketing triages, extracts and produces.

### Editorial intelligence
Titan experts surface issues. Marketing also monitors the industry. Monthly/reactive expert calls turn the strongest issues into content.

### Events
Titan provides sufficient notice of events, launches and opportunities. Marketing defines the capture plan before the day.

### Product releases
Titan supplies structured briefs. Marketing does not reverse-engineer new features from informal conversations.

---

## 13. Internal communication requirements

The content system depends on better inside information.

Titan should provide:
- event calendar and known opportunities;
- customer-opportunity spreadsheet;
- live industry/competitor objections from sales/implementation/support;
- product release briefs;
- access to appropriate experts;
- notice of strategically important campaigns at least several weeks ahead where possible.

This is not about increasing process. It is about removing avoidable information silos.

---

## 14. Review rhythm

The system should remain flexible rather than governed by a rigid calendar.

Recommended rhythm:

- **Continuous:** intake triage and reactive opportunities.
- **Weekly:** light editorial/news scan and live-calendar decision making.
- **Monthly:** performance review + expert conversation + campaign/intelligence check.
- **Quarterly:** deeper strategy review against performance, industry movement and business priorities.

Campaigns and breaking industry moments may override planned slots.

# PART VI — MEASUREMENT

## 15. Measurement principles

Do not compare platforms using one blended score.

### LinkedIn
Track:
- median impressions;
- breakout rate;
- shares/comments;
- quality of engagement;
- saves where available;
- performance by content archetype;
- performance by four-reasons-to-care classification.

### TikTok
Track:
- views;
- follower growth;
- shares;
- saves;
- comments;
- watch/completion where available;
- breakout rate;
- performance of native entertainment vs proof vs educational content.

### Instagram
Track:
- reach/views;
- follower growth;
- saves/shares/comments once reliably ingested;
- which repurposed/native formats outperform baseline.

### YouTube
Track:
- views over time;
- watch time;
- retention;
- subscribers;
- long-tail growth.

### Brand awareness proxy signals
Because direct attribution to demos/adoption is not available, use:
- relevant follower growth;
- repeated engagement from pharmacy professionals;
- organic UGC/trends inspired by Titan;
- customers/industry figures creating content because Titan is active in the space;
- mentions/reposts/shares by recognised industry people/accounts;
- event/social recognition;
- growth in breakout frequency.

### Category authority proxy signals
Track where possible:
- thoughtful comments from operators/sector figures;
- shares/reposts of Titan analysis;
- saves of useful material;
- invitations/requests for Titan commentary or participation;
- recurring engagement with editorial-intelligence content;
- third parties citing/referencing Titan’s framing;
- high performance of issue-led content independent of product promotion.

These are proxies, not perfect attribution.

# PART VII — HARD RULES FOR HUMANS AND AGENTS

## 16. Mandatory content rules

1. **No post ships without a clear reason to care.** It must strongly hit Craft, Value, Connection or Proof.
2. **The hook must make that reason visible quickly.**
3. **Do not publish to fill a slot.**
4. **Do not confuse “testimonial” with proof.** “We love Titan” is weak unless something meaningful sits underneath it.
5. **Do not confuse an opinion with thought leadership.** It must create value or perspective.
6. **Do not lead case studies with Titan.** The pharmacy/operator is the protagonist; Titan is the enabler.
7. **Do not default to feature lists.** Frame product around a problem, consequence or useful workflow.
8. **Do not cross-post blindly.** Decide whether the idea belongs on each platform and adapt accordingly.
9. **Do not force product into entertainment.** Some TikToks can exist solely to serve pharmacy culture and audience growth.
10. **Do not repeatedly repurpose the same customer quote as if it is a new idea.** Derivatives must have distinct editorial jobs.
11. **Do not run generic countdown/event-admin posts unless they contain a substantive reason to care.**
12. **Do not use cryptic teaser copy where the payoff is absent.**
13. **Do not create product-launch content without adequate internal product truth/briefing.**
14. **Do not expose patient-identifiable information.**
15. **Do not make unsupported claims from metrics or infer causation where only correlation exists.**
16. **Titan PMR and Titanverse are connected layers of one ecosystem.** Do not present them as disconnected brands.
17. **PharmAppy is outside the primary scope of this strategy.**
18. **Use Titan’s own performance data before generic social-media doctrine.** External algorithm research can inform hypotheses, not override first-party evidence.

## 19. Q4 tests

Treat these as tests, not immutable policy.

1. **LinkedIn selectivity:** publish only when the editorial gate is passed; assess whether median reach and breakout rate recover without forcing volume.
2. **Native entertainment:** test approximately £1,000/month of creator/content-production budget if approved; assess views, shares, saves and follower growth.
3. **Case-study system:** target one genuinely strong hero every 6-8 weeks, each linked to a strategically relevant industry topic.
4. **Editorial intelligence:** minimum monthly expert session + reactive calls; assess performance of timely issue-led content.
5. **Intake quality:** train colleagues and assess whether better questions/photos create stronger usable stories.
6. **Platform-native derivatives:** measure whether one source story performs better when rebuilt for each platform rather than simply reformatted.
7. **Titanverse support:** identify what internal expertise, customer proof and product clarity is needed to create stronger Titanverse content rather than increasing output for its own sake.

## 20. What this strategy is not

It is not:
- a promise to publish X times every week;
- a list of social hacks;
- a mandate to make everything polished;
- a mandate to make everything educational;
- a mandate to mention Titan in every post;
- a plan to chase every trend;
- a substitute for internal product/customer intelligence;
- proof that the 2026 LinkedIn decline was caused by posting frequency.

It is a system for consistently creating content that people have a reason to care about.
