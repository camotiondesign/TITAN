## Executive Summary

We have been growing, but the machine is manual, the data was misleading us, and reach per post is diluting even while total engagement holds.

**The problem.** Titan's LinkedIn page has scaled from 3,370 followers in mid-2024 to 6,128 in mid-2026, an 82 percent rise. Impressions, reach, and engagement all followed. The picture looks healthy. Underneath, three things are quietly broken. First, the engagement rate we have been reading in the dashboard is roughly a third of the real number, a mapping bug in the analytics pipeline. Second, roughly half the videos we shipped in the last year are performing at or below noise, dragging the video median down without contributing anything. Third, the shapes that reliably earn the most reach on LinkedIn (question-opener hooks, named-operator video) are used in a small minority of posts, so the platform's own bias is under-monetised. We have been running a strong content operation with the instrument panel wired wrong.

**The results.** An operating system, not another strategy revision. Content flows through seven stages — Intake, Classify, Draft, Publish, Measure, Extend, Review — each with a named owner, a tool, and a leverage claim. Killing the 79 below-par video types lifts the video median by 27 percent at zero production cost. Making question-opener hooks the house standard adds a 1.6x free reach uplift. Extending unused hero footage (Asif, Akbar, Malia, Pritee — already shot, already paid for) ships four full campaigns before Christmas. A subagent stack in Cam's voice removes the last-mile bottleneck (caption writing, campaign shaping, folder organising) so Cam can hold the strategic seat. Wahid gets a quarterly quantified review cycle, not a monthly narrative.

**The budget.** Modest. A part-time creative producer (2 to 3 days per week), studio kit reset (lighting and audio for Studio OS intake shoots), and agent infrastructure (Claude platform + the ongoing subagent build). Specific figures to be agreed with Cam.

**The timeline.** Q4 2026 stands the operating system up. Q1 2027 runs the first full cycle end to end.

## Target Audience

Four personas, carried forward from the 2025 segmentation and rewritten with 2026 nuance from the v7 framework.

### Persona 1 — The Pharmacy Owner

**Who they are.** Independent owner or multi-site operator. 11,500 pharmacies across the UK. Family businesses, second-generation operators, first-time acquirers. They open Sundays. They stayed open in the pandemic.

**What they care about, in order.** Cash flow this month. Their team not burning out. Getting the workflow off paper before it kills them. Doing more clinical work without hiring more people. What the sector news actually means for their business.

**How they consume.** LinkedIn feed for 10 to 15 minutes a day, mostly on mobile, mostly on the sofa in the evening. Sector newsletters (P3, C+D, CPCS bulletins) skimmed at breakfast. WhatsApp groups where the real trade happens, owner to owner, invitation only. YouTube on evenings and weekends when they will actually watch a 20-minute story.

**What they do not want.** Feature demos. "Book a demo today." Polished vendor marketing. They smell it in the first line.

**What they want.** Other pharmacy owners on camera telling them how they did the thing. Real numbers. Real mistakes. Real workflow.

**How Titan helps.** Named-operator case studies. Advocacy on the issues they care about. A voice on their side of the trade.

### Persona 2 — The Pharmacist

**Who they are.** Employed pharmacists, locum pharmacists, dispensing pharmacists. Directly affected by whatever PMR they are handed. They do not usually pick the system but they live in it every day and they have loud opinions.

**What they care about.** Usability. Speed. Fewer clicks. More time with patients, less time troubleshooting the software. Not being blamed when it breaks.

**How they consume.** LinkedIn during dispensing quiet periods. Sector newsletters. YouTube for training and CPD. TikTok increasingly for peer content.

**What they do not want.** Anything that sounds like management propaganda.

**What they want.** Honest talk about workflow. Peer voices they trust. Content that acknowledges the day-to-day reality.

**How Titan helps.** Cultural posts, community content, technician spotlights, workflow storytelling. The Weekly Rhythm's Friday Personality slot is the pharmacist slot.

### Persona 3 — The Dispenser or Counter Staff

**Who they are.** The people running the counter, the MDS trays, the CD register. Often longest-serving in the building. Not on LinkedIn much.

**What they care about.** Reliable tools. Not being interrupted with software that does not work. Being able to help patients quickly.

**How they consume.** TikTok, Instagram Reels, Facebook (for the older cohort). Word of mouth in the shop. Rarely LinkedIn.

**How Titan helps.** UGC content and short-form video that features them. The @the.iv.pharmacist wave is the format that reaches this audience. Not a LinkedIn play.

### Persona 4 — Industry Leader

**Who they are.** NHS decision-makers, pharmacy body figures (RPS, NPA, PDA), sector journalists, healthcare tech investors. Small numerical audience, disproportionate downstream influence on adoption and policy.

**What they care about.** The direction of pharmacy. Where the money is flowing. Which operators are worth backing. Whether the sector is moving fast enough on services and AI.

**How they consume.** LinkedIn heavily. Long-form written pieces. Podcasts. Sector conferences.

**How Titan helps.** Hooman-led thought leadership drumbeat. Sector-thesis carousels. Advocacy-with-action posts. Named authority voices (Wahid on AI, Hooman on economics, Tariq on direction). The Wednesday Perspective slot is the industry-leader slot.

## Social Media Audit

### The three-year story

**2024 baseline.** 3,370 followers. 5.28 percent post ER (as reported). 112,145 impressions Jan-Aug. The platform was working, the pattern was not yet formed.

**2025 breakout.** Named-operator case studies landed. The 1,000th pharmacy multi-image. Priory Pharmacy hero (Prabjaudt) at 6,727 impressions and 105 reactions. Total impressions +71.6 percent, page reach +49.2 percent, post engagement rate +410 percent, follower base +45.6 percent. The engine started running.

**2026 consolidation.** 6,128 followers. Median 17 reactions per post, median 854 impressions per post, median real ER 6.87 percent. Per-post reach down 33 percent year on year while total volume rose 25 percent. The story here is not decline. It is dilution. We are shipping more posts and each one is reaching fewer people, while the total engagement holds roughly flat. This is a leverage problem, not a talent problem.

### Data integrity — engagement rate mapping bug

The corrected picture is materially different from what the dashboards have been showing. The engagement rate field in `titan-linkedin.json` was mapped incorrectly against the raw LinkedIn export, understating real ER by roughly a third. The real Titan median ER is 6.5 to 11 percent depending on window and format, not the 1 to 2 percent the old dashboard suggested. Fix is in flight. Every metric in this document uses the corrected number.

**Practical consequence.** Decisions we came close to making on the wrong data — for example, "our engagement is bad, we need a rebuild" — do not apply. The engagement is good. The problem is elsewhere.

### Platform reach ranking (corrected, Q3 2026)

| Platform | Share of total reach | Read |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | ~45% | Primary channel. Owns owner and industry-leader personas. |
| TikTok | ~40% | Larger than assumed. Reaches pharmacist and dispenser personas. Under-invested. |
| Instagram | ~10% | Secondary. Reels format worth keeping light. |
| Facebook | ~5% | Reels averaging 36 views per post. Kill candidate. |

### Video dilution finding

Of 164 videos shipped in the last twelve months, 79 (48 percent) performed at or below the noise floor for their platform. Killing that bottom half of the video mix lifts the video median reach by 27 percent at zero production cost. That is the single largest free lever in the entire operation.

The below-par videos cluster in identifiable types: generic "Titan is amazing" cutdowns, pre-event branded countdown videos, feature-explainer cutdowns without a named human, Facebook Reels in general, third-plus cuts of any single case study.

### Hook shape finding

Cross-checked 136 recent posts. 18 used a question-opener hook. Those 18 averaged 1.63x the reach of the statement-opener posts on LinkedIn specifically. The uplift is smaller on TikTok (native content patterns differ) and effectively noise on Facebook.

**Practical consequence.** Question-opener becomes the house default on LinkedIn caption openers. Statement openers require an editorial reason.

### Cross-platform correlation of same asset

Winners do not travel. A named-operator hero that lands at 11 percent ER on LinkedIn regularly lands at floor-level engagement on TikTok, and vice versa. This kills the "shoot once, post everywhere" mental model and replaces it with "shoot twice, repurpose deliberately".

### The pipeline in one line

**Intake → Classify → Draft → Publish → Measure → Extend → Review.**

Seven stages. Each has an owner, a tool, and a leverage claim. Every piece of content we ship flows through this pipeline. Every claim in the "budget ask" section maps back to a specific stage.

### Stage 1 — Intake

**What it does.** Every piece of raw content enters through one funnel. Three sources feed it: Studio OS intake portal (Nas and on-site staff upload directly), Cam-run shoots (case studies, events, thought leadership), and UGC watch (spontaneous customer content like @the.iv.pharmacist).

**Owner.** Cam (portal + shoots), Intake Curator subagent (UGC watch).

**Tool.** Studio OS intake portal — shipped this weekend. Ashley Matthews' Baxenden batch went through as `cmssqupnx000004l7w7zv7uag` and is Monday's post. Live proof of "no-crew capture → publishable content" mechanic.

**Leverage claim.** Intake portal removes 4 to 6 hours per week of "chase the implementer for context" work. UGC watch catches things Cam would not otherwise see. Cam gets the raw material without being physically at every site.

### Stage 2 — Classify

**What it does.** Every intake gets tagged three ways: production tier (hero / campaign / stocking filler / evergreen), shape (video / multi-image / carousel / quote card), protagonist (named operator / Hooman / cultural / advocacy).

**Owner.** Intake Curator subagent proposes classification, Cam confirms.

**Tool.** Studio OS taxonomy + Notion pipeline.

**Leverage claim.** Kills the "should I even use this?" decision loop. Portal batches with weak input get downgraded automatically to stocking filler or archived. Cam is no longer the classifier of every piece, only the confirmer.

### Stage 3 — Draft

**What it does.** Captions, campaign shapes, sequence planning, evergreen extension briefs. All drafted in Cam's voice by the Comms Agent, following the editorial frameworks below.

**Owner.** Comms Agent v1 (spec being written now — first of a subagent stack: Content Ops, Delivery, Finance, Intake Curator to follow). Cam is the editorial approver.

**Tool.** Claude platform, Cam-voice prompt system, editorial framework library.

**Editorial frameworks.** Two frameworks anchor the draft stage.

*Idea-First Editorial.* The customer is the proof, not the premise. Open with the sharpest thing the customer said. Attribute properly. Frame what it proves. No vendor-announcement openers.

*Fast Company protagonist hierarchy.* Kicker plus headline plus deck plus ironic-reversal deck sentence. Name the person in the headline only when the name carries recognition weight. When it does not, name their role and shop and let the story land. This is the standard being adopted across Titan editorial content.

**Leverage claim.** Draft stage was the last-mile bottleneck. Comms Agent takes 60 to 80 percent of caption-writing off Cam. Cam still writes anything sector-sensitive, anything hero, anything for Hooman. Everything else is drafted by agent, approved by Cam, out the door.

### Stage 4 — Publish

**What it does.** Cam-approved, scheduled via Notion Social directly to LinkedIn / TikTok / Instagram / Facebook Reels (until FB is killed).

**Owner.** Cam (final approval), Notion (scheduling).

**Tool.** Notion editorial board with 220+ post pipeline, integrated with Notion Social. LinkedIn API integration shipping end of October removes Metricool dependency.

**Leverage claim.** Publish is not a bottleneck. Keeping it simple keeps it that way.

### Stage 5 — Measure

**What it does.** Every post gets scored on the real metric set: real ER, reach per post, dilution index (share of below-par posts in the last 30 days), hook compliance (share of LinkedIn posts using question-openers), hero extension count (are we shipping the follow-up cuts we planned), UGC volume (spontaneous tagged content per month).

**Owner.** Cam owns the monthly digest, Wahid owns the quarterly review.

**Tool.** LinkedIn API integration (shipping end of Oct), Studio OS metrics pipeline, monthly digest template.

**Leverage claim.** Fixes the mapping bug. Gives Wahid the same numbers Cam sees. Ends the "which dashboard is right" question. The dilution index turns the "kill below-par videos" instinct into a monthly discipline.

### Stage 6 — Extend

**What it does.** Every hero shoot produces bank material. The Extend stage systematically republishes: unused interview footage from Asif (Safys), Akbar (Drayton Prime), Malia and Pritee (full transcripts in `_interviews-raw/titan/case-studies/raw/`), evergreen quote drops from case study archives 3 to 6 months after original hero (Prabjaudt at 183 days post-hero landed 2,573 impressions and 49 reactions — highest-leverage low-effort slot on the page).

**Owner.** Comms Agent proposes extensions from the interview library, Cam approves and briefs the producer.

**Tool.** Interview transcript library, evergreen quote extraction pipeline, republishing calendar in Notion.

**Leverage claim.** Four hero campaigns already paid for, sitting unshipped. Ships four campaigns Q4 with zero new shoot cost. The single biggest ROI move in the plan.

### Stage 7 — Review

**What it does.** Quarterly, quantified, Wahid-facing. Reads: what was the plan, what shipped, what worked, what did not, what the numbers say, what we are changing for next quarter. Not a narrative. A quantified review with the same metric set each time.

**Owner.** Cam authors, Wahid reads and signs off next-quarter direction.

**Tool.** Quarterly review template (this document is the seed for it).

**Leverage claim.** Wahid gets a real review cycle instead of a monthly update he does not have time to read. Decisions get made at cadence with data on the table.

### The subagent stack

Comms Agent v1 spec being written today. This is the first of a stack. The rollout order:

1. **Comms Agent** (Q4) — captions, campaign shapes, evergreen extension proposals in Cam's voice
2. **Intake Curator** (Q4) — classifies portal batches, watches for UGC, downgrades weak intakes
3. **Content Ops** (Q1 2027) — production tracking, deadline surfacing, delivery hand-offs
4. **Delivery** (Q1 2027) — client-facing status updates, publish confirmations
5. **Finance** (Q1 2027) — retainer allocation, spend tracking against the plan
6. **Studio OS Curator** — richer intake triage as portal volume grows

Each agent has a spec doc, is versioned, and gets a quarterly review of its own. Cam owns the voice fidelity check.

## Content Strategy — Pillars and Rhythm

### The positioning triad

**Proof (Titan PMR).** Named-operator case studies. Customer stories. Concrete before-and-after numbers. The Monday slot.

**Possibility (Titanverse).** Future-of-services content. Clinical model shift. Where the pharmacy business is heading. The Wednesday slot.

**Leadership (drumbeat).** Sector commentary, advocacy, Hooman thought-leadership, Tariq direction pieces. The Friday slot plus reactive.

### The Hybrid Pharmacy narrative

The story that ties Proof, Possibility and Leadership together. Titan PMR frees the pharmacist's time. Titanverse makes that time valuable. Together they define the Hybrid Pharmacy — calm dispensing meets clinical and commercial freedom. Every pillar post lives inside this narrative.

### The Weekly Rhythm

Monday = Proof. Wednesday = Perspective. Friday = Personality.

Same three-beat every week. Audiences feel the rhythm even when they do not consciously notice it. Reactive posts and event windows override but do not replace.

### Monthly production model

**Shoot twice.** One Titan PMR case study, one Titanverse case study, per month. Same crew day if geographically possible.

**Repurpose relentlessly.** Each shoot produces one hero video, one multi-image visit post, one pull-quote card, one carousel to YouTube, one long-form YouTube video, plus bank material for one evergreen quote drop 3 to 6 months later.

**Layer evergreen.** Hooman thought-leadership, sector-thesis carousels, advocacy-with-action, cultural moments.

**Fill with reactive.** Sector news commentary within 48 hours of breaking.
