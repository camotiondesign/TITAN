# LinkedIn Comments Audit for Titan PMR

Primary research from the Titan PMR LinkedIn comment corpus, 16 December 2024 to 30 March 2026. Written to shape audience strategy, not to describe it.

Sources: 151 per-post `comments.md` files under `posts/linkedin/titan/published/*/comments.md` (rich fields: role, connection, reply threads, reactions, impressions), plus `exports/titan-linkedin.json` (author, text) as fallback for a small handful of posts without a comments file. No live scraping was needed. All Titan PMR / Invatech Health staff comments (Nas Khaliq, Sajid Ramzan, Hooman Ghalamkari, Wahid Muhammad, Tariq Muhammad, Jeff Shelley, Dan Khaliq and others) are excluded from the customer / prospect / partner analysis but counted separately where relevant.

Scope limitation, up front. The corpus is thin. Total non-staff comment volume is 174 across 106 posts and roughly twenty months. That is less than one substantive customer voice per week. The comments are also front-loaded: the archive stops in late March 2026 while posts continue to July, meaning three months of the most recent, highest-cadence period (post-TitanUp 26) sits unscraped. Read every finding with that in mind. Where a pattern rests on two or three quotes rather than twenty, that is called out.

The comment corpus is not a mirror of the Trustpilot corpus. Trustpilot was 200 pharmacists writing about a switch. LinkedIn is 174 comments dominated by three or four industry-adjacent accounts saying "great" or "love this", with a much smaller substantive layer sitting underneath. Titan's active LinkedIn audience is not the same population as its Trustpilot reviewer base and behaves differently.

---

## Executive summary

The single loudest fact is that engagement on Titan PMR's LinkedIn is thin and structurally skewed. 213 of 359 posts (59 per cent) received no comment at all. The 174 non-staff comments that do exist are dominated by two individual accounts, Sunil Chandarana / AlphaRize (35 comments across 33 posts) and Balal Anwar (24 across 24 posts), whose contributions are almost always three to six words of encouragement rather than substance. Together those two accounts account for 34 per cent of the entire customer-side comment volume. Strip them out and Titan is looking at roughly 115 comments in twenty months.

The second loudest fact is that the actual customers, the pharmacists who buy and use Titan, hardly ever comment. Where Trustpilot is thick with switching stories from named owners, LinkedIn is thick with peer-industry commentary from consultants, automation partners, tech founders and event organisers. Named Titan customers appear only occasionally, and when they do it is usually to say thank you on a post that is already about them. The audience Titan trains on Trustpilot vocabulary is not the audience answering the comment box.

The third fact is that the comment mix is a Meditech partnership channel as much as a Titan channel. Nineteen of the substantive comments explicitly co-brand Titan with Meditech UK & I (Aron Garner, Balal Anwar, Cyrus Hodivala). This is not accidental. It is a small automation-partner ecosystem talking to itself in public, and it explains why the word "automation" (22 mentions) massively outweighs anything from the trust register. Trustpilot's language is "held our hand" and "left on time". LinkedIn's language is "innovation", "game changer", "workflow", "leading the way".

The fourth fact is that Titan replies. Of the 106 posts with at least one non-staff comment, roughly 40 posts have a nested Titan PMR reply. That is a strong response rate for a brand page and worth protecting. It is also the place where most of the substantive back-and-forth happens: prospects, partners and industry commentators say something, and Titan answers.

The single biggest content opportunity in the next ninety days is not another feature post. It is designing posts that make it safe for a named pharmacist customer, not a partner consultant, to leave a two-sentence comment. Sajid Ramzan's family-life video (25 Feb 2026) is the working prototype. It pulled a real reply from a recruiter about pharmacist burnout and got a rare emotional payoff. Every other post that tries to sell "the Titan Way" pulls "Impressive" from AlphaRize and nothing else.

A few observations that matter more than the ranking.

First, the top of the list is not representative of the customer base. Sunil Chandarana / AlphaRize and Balal Anwar together contributed 59 of 174 comments (34 per cent). Neither speaks in the customer voice Trustpilot captured. Their comments cluster in the "encouragement" and "automation partner" themes, not the "held our hand" or "left on time" themes. If Titan mistakes them for representative customer signal, it will write to the wrong audience.

Second, there is no competitor PMR staff presence in the data at all. Zero comments from anyone identifiable as ProScript, RxWeb, Cegedim, PharmacyX, EMIS, Sonar or Positive Solutions staff. The category is quiet on Titan's public posts. This is either evidence that Titan is not yet a threat competitors feel they need to answer, or evidence that they are choosing not to engage in public. Third, real Titan customers who comment tend to comment on their own case study. Prabjaudt Channa (Priory), Jeet Vadodaria (Medichem), Sagar Lungiwala (Prescot Way), Kanav Sikka (own testimonial), and Rahul Puri (own carousel and BTS multi-image) all comment on posts that feature them. They do not tend to comment on posts about other customers. The exception is Balal Anwar, who tags himself into anyone else's story.

Fourth, the active prospect signal is very small: Romil Patel on ProScript, Makinder Suri asking NMS integration questions, Sagar Lungiwala pre-adoption, Pharmlogic asking about private-market reactions. That is four identifiable prospects in twenty months. Titan's public LinkedIn is not currently a lead-capture surface. It is a community and partner surface.

Fifth, an "active audience" list built off this corpus alone would be tiny. If Titan wants to build one, it needs a scrape run that goes wider than post-comment authors (post likers, video viewers, profile visitors, in-DM conversations) and it needs the March-to-August 2026 window filled in.

   Pharmlogic, on the 2025-08-27 Mounjaro pricing post.
   Content-brief question: **what happens to private-clinic revenue and workflow when a headline weight-loss product's price shifts?** The comment says "[Reply content not provided]" in the archive, suggesting there may be a private-market answer sitting on LinkedIn that was not captured; needs re-scrape to confirm.

3. "TITAN PMR is that a Meditech UK & I robot?"
   Aron Garner, National Consultant Meditech UK & I, 2025-08-21 Medichem visit.
   Rhetorical, but signals a small awkwardness worth naming: robot partners want visible credit on Titan posts and one of them noticed they did not get it.

4. "How are you going to top last year's amazing stand?"
   Debbie Jones, Head of Social Care Partnerships PillSorted, on the 2025-09-02 Pharmacy Show announcement.
   Not a product question, but a public expectations question with 200-plus impressions on it and a very quotable reply available. The thread got a comedy answer from Tas Bhatti and nothing from Titan.

Beyond these formal questions, there is also a broader unanswered thread that Carwyn Jones opens across three separate posts. Wales rollout, Titan Mobile underuse, "we could do with a refresh after our first training". Each individual reply from Titan is fine; the pattern across posts is that a real Titan customer is publicly saying they are getting 10 per cent of the product's value, and Titan is thanking them for the interest rather than commissioning a training-refresh video.

Content briefs that fall out of these unanswered questions:

- A "we're using 10 per cent of what Titan can do" retention series: onboarding-mode training refresh for existing customers, with Carwyn Jones as the archetype
- A private-market pricing-shock response piece: what Titan and Titanverse do when a headline drug price moves, with Mounjaro as the trigger example
- A phone-question workload piece for the phone-anxiety cluster: what Titanverse's PharmAppy and Prescription Ready do to the twenty-times-a-day interruption pattern
- A Wales rollout status piece, explicit, with dates or with an honest "not yet"
- A recurring house-response for adjacent-market pitches (SteoAI, RxBridge, ClinicRx type comments): either invite them onto the podcast or send them a polite one-line acknowledgement

## Section 6: Signal shifts over time

Note on framing. The brief asks for a pre- and post-TitanUp 25 split. TitanUp 26 (June 2026) is the recent event; TitanUp 25 does not appear as a named event in this repo. The comment scrape also stops at 30 March 2026, ten weeks before TitanUp 26, so a pre- vs post-TitanUp 26 split is impossible from this data. The most usable inflection point in the archive is Pharmacy Show 2025 (12 October 2025), which was Titan's largest event coverage window and produced the highest volume of substantive LinkedIn comments in the corpus. All time-series comments below use that as the split.

### 6.1 Volume shift

Pre-Pharmacy Show 2025 (16 Dec 2024 to 11 Oct 2025): 118 non-staff comments across 75 posts on a base of 160 posts. Roughly 0.74 non-staff comments per post.

Post-Pharmacy Show 2025 (12 Oct 2025 to 30 Mar 2026): 56 non-staff comments across 31 posts on a base of 198 posts. Roughly 0.28 non-staff comments per post.

Comment volume per post more than halved. Post cadence increased. This is a compression, not a growth. Either the community became less responsive as Titan posted more, or Titan's post mix shifted toward formats that pull fewer comments (single-image opinion pieces, short reactive-news posts), or LinkedIn's algorithm suppressed the account after October. The most likely explanation, given the post archive, is a change of format mix. This is worth checking against Cam's own aggregated metrics.

### 6.2 Author shift

Pre-Pharmacy Show 2025 top authors: Sunil Chandarana (19), Balal Anwar (18), AlphaRize (16, same person as Sunil), Aron Garner (3), Romil Patel (3), Sagar Lungiwala (3), Mustafa Joda (3).

Post-Pharmacy Show 2025 top authors: Balal Anwar (6), Abhyuday Daharwal (3), Rahul Puri (2), IPCN (2), Carwyn Jones (2), Hitesh P. (2), Michael Holden (2), Tony Donnelly (2).

Two shifts stand out. First, Sunil Chandarana / AlphaRize almost completely stopped commenting after the Pharmacy Show (35 comments before, 0 after). That is a single-account behaviour change and it explains a large chunk of the volume drop above. Second, the post-Pharmacy Show list is more diverse and slightly more substantive: Carwyn Jones's product questions, Michael Holden's opinion writing, Tony Donnelly's tags-workflow comments, Abigail Barry's ICB endorsement. If AlphaRize's silence is removed from the picture, the post-October audience is arguably higher quality even though it is lower volume.

### 6.3 Theme shift

Pre-Pharmacy Show. Dominated by short encouragement, automation-partner co-signal, and Meditech tagging.

Post-Pharmacy Show. A larger share of comments carry an argument. Kirit Shah on GPhC jurisdiction, Michael Holden on sustainable pharmacy models, HubRx on the hub-and-spoke law change, Piyushkumar Patel on nomination-switching patients, Gemma Brice on family time. These are people making a point, not people typing "love this".

That is a real shift worth respecting. The post-October comment box became a slightly more serious room. The posts that opened that door are the reactive-news carousels (bulk nomination, 158 million clawback, GTIN barcodes, NHS 10-year plan) and the emotional customer stories (Sajid HOD, Prab quote, Anna Matthews review). Not the feature explainers.

### 6.4 Topics that went quiet

Two topics that had comment traction pre-Pharmacy Show and appear to have gone silent after.

Merch and brand culture. The March 2025 Titan merch launch pulled comments from four separate accounts. No later merch-adjacent post pulled comparable traction.

The 1000th-pharmacy milestone. July 2025 had multi-thread congratulations posts (Priory longform, Titan 1000th milestone, montage). Nothing later in the corpus has repeated that milestone energy, even Titanup registration content.

### 6.5 Topics that went louder

Product and pharmacy-model criticism. The August 2025 clinical-checks post (Tas Bhatti's "copy and paste PMR providers" quote) is the first competitor-named substantive comment in the entire archive. The 2026-01-15 bulk-nomination post produced the first substantive regulatory-argument thread. February 2026 posts about Prab leaving pharmacy and Sajid's family life produced the first genuine emotional replies. The comment layer is shifting from "cheerleading" toward "argument".

### 6.6 Silences that persist

Nothing in the data suggests any change to the following silences from Trustpilot:

- No pricing conversation. Zero non-staff comments discuss what Titan costs.
- No contract or lock-in conversation. Zero non-staff comments discuss switching cost, tie-ins or negotiation.
- No downtime or reliability conversation. Zero non-staff comments raise outages or uptime.
- No junior-pharmacist voice. Every named non-staff commenter across the corpus is an owner, superintendent, director, consultant, or industry figure. The dispensing floor is absent from the comment box.
- No serious contrary voice. Not a single negative or critical comment about Titan itself sits in the corpus, in either window. Even Carwyn Jones's "using 10 per cent of Titan's capability" is framed as praise.

The Trustpilot silences and the LinkedIn silences match. That is a pattern worth naming. Titan's public surfaces are both, in different ways, avoiding the honest conversation about price, contract friction, junior-pharmacist experience, and product limitations.

## Section 7: Cross-reference to Trustpilot

Written to sit alongside `trustpilot-linguistic-analysis.md`. The two corpora are the same brand's public voice on two surfaces and they should be read together.

Where they agree.

- The named implementation team story surfaces on both, but tilted differently. Trustpilot puts Nas at the centre. LinkedIn puts Sajid and Tariq at the centre.
- The emotional payoff, when it appears, is the same: time back, family time, pharmacist stress, leaving on time. It just appears rarely on LinkedIn.
- The pharmacy-under-pressure frame ("survival", "community pharmacy needs this") is present on both, worded slightly more institutionally on LinkedIn (Michael Holden, Sanjeev Panesar, Abigail Barry).

Where they disagree.

- Trustpilot is customer-owner voice. LinkedIn is partner-consultant voice.
- Trustpilot vocabulary is "held our hand", "smooth", "helpful", "patient". LinkedIn vocabulary is "innovation", "leading the way", "game changer", "automation".
- Trustpilot names implementation staff (Nas, Atique, Nour, Maj, Nabil, Vicky, Sarah, Jeff). LinkedIn names leadership (Tariq, Sajid, Hooman, Wahid). The implementation team is invisible on LinkedIn.
- Trustpilot is thick with switch stories. LinkedIn has zero switch-story confessions in the entire corpus.
- Trustpilot silences on price. LinkedIn silences on price. Both point to the same buyer psychology.

Implication for strategy. If Titan wants to move its LinkedIn comment mix closer to its Trustpilot voice, the mechanism is not more feature posts. It is more posts that (a) name and film the implementation team, (b) tell a switch story from the customer's own mouth, (c) invite an emotional reply by putting an emotional stake on the page. Every Trustpilot pattern has a working prototype somewhere in the LinkedIn archive (Sajid HOD family life for emotion; the Anna Matthews review carousel for switch story; the Malpas Pharmacy Emma testimonial for named team member). Titan has done it. It just has not done it often.

## Section 8: Practical recommendations

Written short. Six moves.

1. Stop treating the AlphaRize / Balal comment layer as customer signal. Both are valuable community upkeep and neither is data on what pharmacists actually feel. Report on comment volume with those two accounts stripped, alongside the raw count.

2. Fill the March-to-August 2026 comment gap. Three months of Titan's highest-cadence, event-heavy content window (TitanUp 26, Future Pharmacist Conference, board-level appointments) is not in this dataset. A scraper run over the last 30 to 40 most-commented posts in that window will materially change what this audit sees.

3. Design at least one comment-inviting post per fortnight. The prototypes exist: Sajid HOD family life (emotional invitation), 158 million clawback (regulatory invitation), the Sagar 4x growth carousel (customer-adjacent celebration). Feature explainer posts and workflow diagrams get partner comments only.

4. Turn Carwyn Jones's public admission ("we're using about 10 per cent of Titan's capability") into a training-refresh series aimed at existing customers. This is the only concrete retention gap the comment corpus reveals.

5. Publish a house response for adjacent-market pitches in the comment box. RxBridge, SteoAI, ClinicRx, PharmData, PharmAppy, PillSorted, HubRx, Pharmacy Mentor, Robson Pharmacy Media all appear. Some are answered, most are ignored. A consistent short reply protects the brand voice and signals a curated stage.

6. Bring the implementation team onto LinkedIn the way they already live on Trustpilot. Nas is named 84 times on Trustpilot and twice in this whole LinkedIn corpus. Filming her, Atique, Nour, Maj, Vicky, Nabil, Sarah and Jeff as recurring on-camera presences would import the Trustpilot brand into a channel where currently only the founders and the sales lead carry it.
