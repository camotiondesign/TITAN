**Status:** v1, based on direct reading of the source articles uploaded to this project. Add new articles to the archive and run an update pass when Tariq publishes new columns.

- For framework carousels → use the published frameworks

## 1. The six signature analogies

These are Tariq's distinctive voice devices. Each pass the logos-stripped test instantly. Each is a LinkedIn carousel or single-image post waiting to be written. **None are currently on LinkedIn at the time of this file.**

### Bricklayer analogy
**Source:** *Going Private*, Pharmacy Business, October 2024
**Tariq's words (verbatim):** *"We know remuneration influences behaviour. If I have a construction company and I pay a bricklayer a fee per brick to supply and fit, I suspect he will source them from the cheapest supplier and lay as many bricks as he possibly can. If one day, I reduce the fee such that it barely covers the cost of the brick, at a time when bricks are in short supply and labour costs have gone up, oh and I take some money off him for the profit he made on bricks he supplied the previous year, I suspect he will tell me to stick my job where the sun doesn't shine."*

**The point:** remuneration drives behaviour. The NHS pharmacy contract is the equivalent of paying the bricklayer less than the cost of the brick. The current behaviour (pharmacies pivoting to private services or closing) is a rational economic response, not a moral failing.

**Application:** anywhere a post needs to explain why pharmacy is shifting from NHS-dispensing to private-services. The bricklayer is a perfect analogy for B2B audiences who get cost-of-supply economics intuitively.

### Dentist analogy
**Source:** *Going Private*, Pharmacy Business, October 2024
**Tariq's words (verbatim):** *"In 2006 something significant happened in the dental profession. The labour government of the day introduced a new contract which created an arbitrary scale for dental work, known as a Unit of Dental Activity (or UDA) which had no bearing on the actual work involved, and targets were introduced for practices. What followed was a rejection of this draconian system which resulted in sharp decline in the provision of NHS dentistry and the growth of private dental services. Today, NHS dentistry is in a desperate state and inaccessible for the vast majority of the population. Some may look at dentists and say they chose profit over patients but this would be a failure to understand the sequence of events. Dentists were OK providing NHS services but the conditions were created through their NHS contract that gave them no choice but to find their livelihoods elsewhere."*

**The point:** pharmacy is on the same path as dentistry post-2006. The contract makes NHS services unsustainable; private services become the rational response. This is not a moral judgment; it's an economic prediction with twenty years of evidence behind it.

**Application:** the strongest available framing for the items-to-services shift thesis. Carousel, video voiceover, blog post, founder talk. Particularly powerful because the audience knows the dental story is true.

### Tesla analogy
**Source:** *How tech can unite pharmacy*, Pharmacy Business, November 2021
**Tariq's words (verbatim):** *"The idea that a large portion of prescriptions could come down the spine, miss the pharmacist completely and continue the journey with pharmacy staff, is unnerving to say the least... It was the same expression I had when I first pressed auto-steer on a Tesla and let go of the steering wheel."*

And: *"Unlike Remote Supervision, AI does not advocate the absence of a pharmacist on the premises. On the contrary, Titan.X has been programmed so that a RP must be present at all times and automatically shuts down if its stringent protocols are not followed. Many built-in standards ensure the RP remains actively in control over dispensing, much like sitting in the driver's seat whilst the car is on auto-drive."*

**The point:** AI clinical checks aren't replacing the pharmacist any more than Tesla auto-steer replaces the driver. The pharmacist stays in control; AI removes the repetitive cognitive load.

**Application:** any post addressing AI fear or "AI is replacing pharmacists" concerns. Works as direct refutation. Pairs well with the 0.12% override stat below.

### Amazon analogy
**Source:** *Rebuilding the foundation of community pharmacy*, Pharmacy Business, June/July 2020
**Tariq's words (verbatim):** *"We need to think of dispensing as production line work. Clinical risk needs to be managed intelligently as above, but supply needs to be treated as supply. If we want to prevent Amazon one day taking our business, we need to start thinking like Amazon now and the PMR needs to support a production-like workflow."*

**The point:** production-line thinking applied to dispensing. Not robots — process design. Amazon doesn't dispense pharmaceuticals by accident; they design the workflow ruthlessly. Pharmacy can do the same.

**Application:** workflow-and-engineering posts. Counters the "more shiny machines" instinct. The Batch Flow story is literally this analogy made real.

### Boiling frog analogy
**Source:** *Pharmacy Survival Guide: Adapt and Change*, Pharmacy Business, June 2024 (also referenced in October 2024 *Going Private*)
**Tariq's words (verbatim):** *"Or is it like the boiling frog story, where the temperature has increased gradually around us that we haven't noticed the government heat slowly killing community pharmacy."*

**The point:** the government has been gradually reducing pharmacy funding for 25 years. No single change has been dramatic enough to trigger a profession-wide response. The cumulative effect is fatal.

**Application:** any post that needs to explain why pharmacy is in crisis even though no single event explains it. Particularly useful for newer pharmacy audiences who weren't around to see the slow squeeze.

### Skyscraper on a weak foundation
**Source:** *Rebuilding the foundation of community pharmacy*, Pharmacy Business, June/July 2020
**Tariq's words (verbatim):** *"The thought of adding more volume, more services and more workload into an environment which can barely cope with what it has, is like adding more floors to a building whose foundation was only meant to be one-story."*

And closing: *"If we proudly want to see pharmacy standing tall in the future as one of the skyscrapers in healthcare, then we best get back to basics and fix our foundation first!"*

**The point:** you cannot build a services pharmacy on a broken dispensary. The dispensary engine has to be fixed first. This is literally the four-layer stack thesis from 2020 — Dispensary as foundation for Clinical Platform.

**Application:** the meta-analogy for the entire Titan thesis. Strong for explaining why PMR (Dispensary) and Titanverse (Clinical Platform) belong together. The stack made visual.

## 2. The published strategic frameworks

Tariq has published multiple multi-point frameworks in print. Each is a carousel-ready structure already in his voice.

### Framework A — Six pillars of pharmacy re-engineering
**Source:** *Rebuilding the foundation of community pharmacy*, June/July 2020

1. **Barcode validation** — scan-to-label dispensing should be mandatory, not optional
2. **Intelligent checking** — AI removes the cognitive load of pointless final checks
3. **Re-distributed workload** — junior staff can dispense safely if process supports them
4. **Production line** — think like Amazon, design the workflow
5. **Paperless** — digital workflow not paper supported by digital
6. **Interoperability** — at minimum, your PMR should connect to everything else you use

**Carousel readiness:** six slides ready-made. Slide 7 = the unified stack tilt as the framework's purpose.

### Framework B — Pharmacy Survival Guide
**Source:** *Pharmacy Survival Guide: Adapt and Change*, June 2024

1. **Let It Go** — the pharmacist doesn't need to do every check. AI and process let dispensing happen safely without them.
2. **Maximise Services** — the lost dispensing income shows up in services. Maximise them.
3. **Start Charging** — for deliveries, MDS, and other services pharmacies have given away for free. Days of free everything are over.
4. **Go Private** — the level of private services pharmacies offer will grow exponentially. Get in.
5. **Connect with Patients** — apps, eMAR, patient-facing tech to take control of the relationship.
6. **Distribute Services** — hub and spoke regulations are coming. Titan pharmacies already do this.

**Carousel readiness:** six slides. The carousel is the framework with one slide per point. Closing slide = the willingness-to-adapt thesis.

### Framework C — Crossroads framing
**Source:** *Going Private*, October 2024

- The current crossroads: keep going on NHS-dispensing-as-survival, or pivot to private services like dentistry did
- The false dream: hoping the NHS contract will one day pay dividends
- The audience that gets it: pharmacies who realised the contract is fundamentally flawed and the government has no money
- The condition for crossing: vision + the right tools (Titan PMR for the dispensary, Titanverse for the services)

**Carousel readiness:** four slides with a clear "where are you on the crossroads" CTA. Pairs naturally with the dentist analogy.

### Framework D — Destination → Dispensary → Clinical Platform → Shopfront
**Source:** *Apps and Tools*, March 2026

The four-layer pharmacy strategic stack:
1. **Your Destination** — what services are you providing? Private or NHS? What's your commercial model? How will you differentiate?
2. **Your Dispensary** — the PMR is the bedrock. Get this right first. (Titan PMR)
3. **Your Clinical Platform** — the system that manages the patient journey from booking through to prescription issue. (Titanverse, plus integrations)
4. **Your Shopfront** — your website and patient app. The new front door. (PharmAppy)

**Carousel readiness:** four slides. **This is the most important framework in the archive** because it makes the unified stack tilt explicit. Should be a permanent pinned carousel on both Titan PMR and Titanverse LinkedIn pages once written.

## 6. Recurring voice patterns

Beyond the analogies and stances, Tariq has consistent micro-patterns worth replicating in brand-voice content where Tariq himself isn't the byline.

### Pattern A — The "every time I visit a Titan pharmacy" frame
Tariq's columns frequently start with him visiting a customer pharmacy and being told something specific. The frame creates immediacy and human texture.

**Example:** *"Every now and then I get a chance to leave the office and visit a few of our Titan pharmacy customers around the country. It's a pleasure to meet business owners and talk about the highs and lows of pharmacy, and Titan of course."* — *Pharmacy Survival Guide*

**Application:** any post about customer success or product roadmap. The "I just got back from..." frame is reusable across video voiceovers and LinkedIn opens.

### Pattern B — The "I remember when..." historical mark
Tariq frequently grounds present-day claims by referencing a specific historical moment in pharmacy. This adds authority and contextualises the current pressure as part of a longer story.

**Examples:**
- *"I remember reading an open letter in 2016 by the then Chancellor, Philip Hammond..."* — *Pharmacy Survival Guide*
- *"When I qualified as a pharmacist, an average pharmacy dispensed 4,000 items per month..."* — *Rebuilding the Foundation*
- *"It doesn't feel that long ago when community pharmacy seemed stuck in the dark ages..."* — *Apps and Tools*

**Application:** any post that needs to make a "things have changed" case. The historical mark gives the change weight.

### Pattern C — The numbered-list framework
Tariq's columns frequently structure around named, numbered points. He's a list thinker. The frameworks in Section 2 all use this structure.

**Application:** carousels. Every Tariq framework converts cleanly to a 5-7 slide carousel.

### Pattern D — Direct contractor-style language
Tariq uses second-person direct address frequently ("you", "your pharmacy", "what you want to offer") rather than abstract third-person.

**Example:** *"If you own a pharmacy business and are looking to move your business to becoming more services led, it's important that you do your homework."*

**Application:** any post addressed to the operator audience. Default to "you" not "pharmacies".

### Pattern E — The aside / parenthetical
Tariq frequently inserts a wry aside in brackets or with a dash. Adds warmth without breaking authority.

**Examples:**
- *"...and we are then added into the mix."*
- *"...oh and I take some money off him..."*
- *"...or should I say, outside the tote."*

**Application:** sparingly. One aside per piece. The voice device works because it's rare.

# TitanUp 26 Extension (added July 2026)

Everything below is extracted verbatim from the TitanUp 26 keynote transcript (7 June 2026, Birmingham). This is the richest single founder-voice artefact in the archive: an hour of Tariq on stage in the register the audience most responds to. It also functions as de facto founder validation of the unified tilt — see the closing declaration.

## 8. The seventh signature analogy — the Supermarket Loss Leader

**Source:** TitanUp 26 keynote, June 2026
**Tariq's words (verbatim):** *"Look at supermarkets. Supermarkets don't make any money out of bread or milk. They have bread and milk, and they put it right at the back of the store, and they make you walk past loads of aisles to get to it, because they know that if they price those things cheap... they will make their money out of you from buying all of the other things that they're selling. So to them bread and milk is a loss leader. Now imagine if we looked at the NHS in that way... instead of looking at the NHS as a source of income that we have to make profit from, what if we think of it as a way that brings a source of opportunity that brings business to our doors?"*

And the qualifier: *"That concept only works if you've got other things to sell them. That's like the supermarket having bread and milk, and all the other shelves are empty. Our supermarket shelves are our private services. We need to stack it high, but don't sell it cheap."*

And the kicker: *"We need to do everything: travel clinics, vaccinations, weight loss, hair loss... testosterone replacement therapy, hormone replacement therapy. Do this, and it will become your financial replacement therapy."*

**The point:** stop treating the NHS as the income line; treat it as the cheapest customer-acquisition channel in healthcare. If a service loses £1,000 a month, reframe it as £1,000 cost-of-acquisition bringing patients to your door — provided the private-services shelves are stocked.

**Application:** the definitive framing for the items-to-services shift at the business-model level. Works as carousel, keynote clip caption, or Titanverse business-platform content. The "financial replacement therapy" pun is Tariq's own and should be attributed, never presented as brand copy.

## 9. The origin story (canonical version, on the record)

Tariq told the full Pharmacy Plus story on stage. The beats, all verbatim-sourced and now publicly citable:

- Built over 20 years: chain of pharmacies, distance-selling operation, hub and spoke, largest care home operator in the UK after Boots. *"My business was dispensing a quarter of a million prescription items a month, and I had a turnover of 30 million pounds a year."*
- October 2012: *"the government announced a change, a cheeky change to the drug tariff... that change wiped 1 million pounds off my bottom line."*
- *"12 months later, the business that I spent 20 years building, Pharmacy Plus, went into administration. 400 souls, my colleagues, my friends, my family lost their jobs. I lost everything."*
- The promise made in the rubble: rebuild under a new model treating independents *"not as competitors but as comrades"* — that company is Invatech Health, that product is Titan, *"and that lesson is profit."*

**Usage rule:** the origin story is Tariq's to tell. Use it in his voice or as directly attributed narrative. Never compress it into a brand boast.

## 10. The profit doctrine (stance lines, verbatim)

- *"Wanting profit doesn't make you greedy. Wanting profit does not make you selfish. Wanting profit is simply admitting the reality of being in business."*
- *"It's not pharmacy first, it's finance first."*
- *"Profit isn't the opposite of care. Profit is what makes care possible."*
- *"Profit is your lifeline... it's like oxygen, without it you die. And just like in a plane where the masks fall in during times of turbulence, you have to help your business before it can help others."*
- *"When you're making profit, people want to lend to you, people want to invest in you... When you don't make profit, the only people interested in your business are the bailiffs and the administrators."*
- *"Profit may not be the thing that we dream of, but profit is the thing that will help your dreams come true."*
- *"Profit times multiple equals valuation... if your profit is zero, it doesn't matter what you multiply that number by, your value is zero."*

- On the P word taboo: *"To this day, if you go to any conference, any pharmacy convention... no one mentions the P word. If it's said you feel dirty."*

## 11. The habits framework (break three, make three)

A complete Tariq framework, stage-tested, carousel-ready:

**Habits to break:**
1. **Stop relying on the NHS.** *"When 95% of your income is coming from a single source that can change its terms overnight, that is not security, that is vulnerability dressed up as stability."*
2. **Stop giving stuff for free.** *"When you give your time, your services, your products for free... what does that say? It's worth nothing. It's worthless. You are worthless."* (He owns his own history here: *"I put my hand up, I did this on an industrial scale."*)
3. **Stop the guesswork.** *"Instinct built on decades of experience is genuinely a very valuable thing, but instinct built without data is just hope, and hope is not a business strategy."*

**Habits to make:**
1. **Optimise income and operations** — endorsements, claims, concessions, uncollected items: *"that's money earned that you are handing back... It's not going to make you millions, but you'll stop the haemorrhage."*
2. **Treat NHS services as loss-stopping, not growth** — *"NHS services is not an additional income... This is income that has been taken out of dispensing and given back to you in the form of services."*
3. **Build new income streams** — the supermarket loss-leader analogy (section 8).

Plus the operating frame: **circle of concern / circle of influence / circle of control.** *"You can't change the NHS, you can't change that contract... that boat has sailed, so stop worrying about it, let it go."* Focus goes to *"that tiny, tiny area of things that you can actually do today, which will make a difference tomorrow."*
