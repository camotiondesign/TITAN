# Titan Voice Canon

The primary voice source for all caption writing. When `/captions` or any drafting task needs to know how Titan sounds, it reads this file, not the datasets. The datasets (titan-linkedin.json, titanverse-linkedin.json, titan-instagram.json, titan-facebook.json) are for performance maths only.

**Why this file exists:** the old method (comments-weighted top quartile from the JSON) broke because half of all posts have zero comments, so the filter degenerated. Voice is a judgement call, not a metric. These captions were selected by a real-engagement composite (comments and shares weighted over reactions, per 1,000 impressions) and then curated by hand. Every entry earns its place.

**How to use it:** read the relevant layer's canon before drafting. Match the job, not the topic. Each entry is annotated with the move that makes it work and, where relevant, the flaw to avoid repeating. The flaws are as instructive as the strengths.

**Status:** v1, built July 2026 from the full merged dataset (Dec 2024 to June 2026). Re-curate quarterly or when a new post clearly beats an entry.

## Part 1 — Titan PMR canon

### 1. The Jaya ecosystem story — customer story, quote-led
*Single image, May 2026. Composite 88.8, 10 comments (top of the entire 2026 dataset for real engagement).*

> "The tools Titan has built support the whole pharmacy ecosystem."
>
> That's Jaya at Marton Road Pharmacy.
>
> And he didn't jump in straight away.
>
> When Titan first launched, his pharmacy was a smaller operation.
> The timing wasn't right.
>
> Now the pressure is different.
>
> Two pharmacies.
> More workload.
> Rising staffing costs.
> More services to deliver.
>
> So this time, Jaya went all in.
>
> ✅ Titan PMR
> ✅ Hub and spoke
> ✅ Titanverse
> ✅ PharmAppy
>
> In nearly four weeks, 500 patients have registered through PharmAppy.
> 200+ orders have already come through the platform.
>
> But the bigger point is this:
>
> Jaya isn't building around one tool.
>
> He's building around the whole pharmacy.

**Why it works:** opens on the customer's verbatim line, not our claim. The honesty beat ("he didn't jump in straight away") does more credibility work than any adjective could — it makes the eventual all-in feel earned. Pressure named in four concrete fragments. Numbers specific and recent. The close lands the stack thesis without naming it.
**The move to steal:** admit the friction in the story. Customers who hesitated and then moved are more persuasive than customers who loved it instantly.

## Part 3 — What the canon teaches (the fingerprint)

Read across all entries, the house voice has a consistent fingerprint. This is what "sounds like Titan" actually means in mechanics:

1. **Verbatim beats paraphrase, always.** The best posts are built around a real person's actual words, imperfections kept ("pretty poor", "absolute nightmare"). If a quote exists, open on it or pivot on it. Never composite quotes across speakers.
2. **The honesty beat.** Admitting friction — the customer who hesitated, the riddle we didn't get, the timing that wasn't right — appears in a disproportionate share of top performers. Confidence that can afford honesty is the house register.
3. **Fragments carry meaning or they go.** Short-line stacking is everywhere in the canon, but each fragment names a distinct concrete thing (Two pharmacies. / More workload. / Rising staffing costs.). Decorative fragment rhythm is the tell; loaded fragment rhythm is the voice.
4. **Numbers are characters.** 40,000 items. 500 patients in four weeks. 15,000 items and home by 5. Three calendars. The number is never decoration; it is usually the protagonist.
5. **The product enters late.** In nearly every canon entry the reader's problem is fully real before Titan appears. Pain first, product as resolution, never product as opener.
6. **British, dry, specific.** "No faff." "So it makes sense that." "Hmm, let us think." Understatement over exclamation. One emoji doing a job beats five doing decoration.
7. **The close trusts the reader.** The strongest endings are a fact, a name, or a short stance. Every time a summarising kicker crept in ("That's a different way of working"), it was the weakest line of an otherwise strong post.

The datasets exist to check whether a new draft clears the closest comparable here. This file exists so the draft sounds like the eight things above.
