# Titan Founder Voices — Wahid and Hooman

Companion file to `tariq-voice-library.md`. Tariq's voice is documented there. This file codifies the two other founder registers captured verbatim at TitanUp 26 (7 June 2026, Birmingham), so that content built on their talks sounds like them and not like a brand paraphrase.

**The standing rule applies to all three:** direct founder voice works; retold founder voice fails. Use their verbatim lines near-verbatim in founder-voiced content. In brand voice, reference rather than replicate.

**Source:** TitanUp 26 keynote transcripts (morning session: Wahid product keynote, Hooman commercial keynote; afternoon session: Oracle reveal). All quotes below are verbatim from the transcripts unless marked as tidied for filler words.

---

## 1. Wahid Muhammad — the Builder register

CTO. The voice of product truth and technical conviction. Where Tariq argues from economics and history, Wahid argues from what the team has actually built. His register is enthusiastic but grounded in process — the enthusiasm is earned by showing the work.

### Signature lines (verbatim, citable)

- **"We don't actually use AI... We don't use AI at all. We build AI. And there is a big, big difference of using AI and building AI."** — the defining Wahid stance. The confession-then-reveal structure ("we've got a bit of a confession") is his native shape.
- **"Your AI is only as good as the data that you train it on."** — on the clinical team manually training models. "It's laborious, it's painful, but it needs to be done."
- **"What I've just shown you is the tip of the iceberg. What I'll be showing you later is the iceberg."** — the Wahid tease. Withholds by naming the withholding.
- **"Level five is absolutely amazing. It is shockingly good."** — note: Wahid is permitted superlatives Tariq never uses, because they arrive after the process has been shown. The order is always evidence, then enthusiasm.
- **"You'll have to switch it on and use it. All I can say is bloody amazing."** — on being asked to prove Level 5 claims. Refuses to argue, points at the product.
- **"Titanverse is your business platform, it's your finance platform, and it is your business in your pocket."** — the repivot line. The single most strategically important sentence from his talks.
- **"Stop the hemorrhage"** — his recurring frame for NHS income leakage (concessions, endorsements, claims). Used at least three times. British spelling in our content: haemorrhage.
- **"Smoke and mirrors"** — on pharmacy being the only industry where revenue and cost of goods are both blurred. "They don't teach you this in business school. They don't teach you this in school at all. We're here to stop that."
- **Oracle framing: "Oracle is the shift from when you stop running a business to when you start talking to your business."**
- **"There is no need for reports. You simply talk."**
- **"You guys are sitting on a mountain of data, all of you, every single one of you."**

### Voice mechanics

- **Confession-then-reveal.** Opens with an apparent negative ("we don't use AI", "we've got a confession") that flips into the stronger claim. This is his version of the curiosity loop and it is distinct from Tariq's stance-first openings.
- **Process as proof.** Names the team (Professor Mohammed Bilal, two PhDs, the in-house solicitor Melissa, MHRA alignment) before making any capability claim. Capability claims that skip the process step do not sound like Wahid.
- **The repeated intensifier.** "Really, really", "big, big", "heavily, heavily". In print, use once per piece at most — it reads as transcription noise beyond that, but a single instance keeps his cadence.
- **Direct instruction closes.** "Install it up, up you go, and off you go." "Now's the time to really start jumping on." He ends segments by telling the audience exactly what to do tonight.
- **Honest about the unknown.** "The only big question mark is the NHS side of things... I can't give an exact date on that." Wahid flags uncertainty explicitly; content in his voice must never claim timelines he refused to give.

### Where the Wahid register belongs
Product truth posts, AI explainers, Oracle content, Level 5 content, roadmap and "what's live now" content, anything where the claim needs engineering credibility rather than business framing.

## 2. Hooman Ghalamkari — the Fair Dealer register

COO. The voice of commercial empathy and pricing fairness. His TitanUp talk was structured as shared dilemma, principles, then theatre — he made the audience negotiate their own discount. The register is warm, self-deprecating, and always framed from the customer's side of the table.

### Signature lines (verbatim, citable)

- **"If you're thriving, then we're thriving."** — principle one of the commercial model. The defining Hooman line.
- **"How are you going to make any money out of NHS services? £17 is what you're going to be offered. Those services are capped."** — commercial realism about NHS consultation fees, setting up why NHS tasks are free in Titanverse.
- **"We will not charge you for those, because we want you to keep your margin, whatever it is. You're not going to make money out of that, so we're not going to make money out of you doing those services."** — the NHS-consultations-free rationale. This is the fairness argument in full.
- **"It has to be fair for you guys, and it has to be fair for Titan, and the model has to be sustainable, so that we can carry on supporting you and we can carry on innovating."** — the three-part commercial principle.
- **"I'm sympathising with you guys... it's like when my wife says she wants to do something really nice for the family, like plan this amazing holiday, and I'm tempted, right, but at the back of my mind I'm thinking, how much is it going to cost me?"** — the self-deprecating relatability open. He begins from the buyer's anxiety, not the seller's pitch.
- **"If you don't like it, don't blame me, blame your representatives"** — the pricing panel joke (the customer pricing committee). His humour always shares credit or deflects it, never punches at the audience.

### Voice mechanics

- **Buyer's-side framing.** Every commercial point is introduced from the customer's fear or maths first. Content in his voice about pricing must open with what it costs the reader, not what it gives Titan.
- **Participatory structure.** "Should it be higher or lower? I can't hear you." He builds the number with the room. In caption form this becomes the genuine question CTA, used honestly.
- **Named accountability.** He names the customer pricing committee members. Fairness claims in his register carry named witnesses.
- **Principles before prices.** He spent longer on the three principles than the numbers. Any commercial content should do the same.

### Where the Hooman register belongs
Offer and pricing content, commitment-offer follow-ups, "why we price it this way" posts, implementation and onboarding content, anything where the reader's suspicion of a sales pitch is the first obstacle.

## 3. Register boundaries (do not blur)

| Situation | Voice |
|---|---|
| Economics, history, profession-level stance, habits, profit doctrine | Tariq (see tariq-voice-library.md) |
| Product capability, AI, roadmap, what's live, engineering credibility | Wahid |
| Pricing, offers, fairness, commercial empathy | Hooman |
| Day-to-day brand posts | Grounded Operator house voice (voice guide), which may *reference* any founder |

A post that quotes one founder must attribute to that founder only. The Wahid/Tariq composite-quote incident (June 2026) is the standing cautionary example: "It's packed, and it's doing it already" merged two speakers and had to be caught by hand. Every founder quote must be traceable to the transcript, verbatim, single-speaker. When in doubt, check `transcript-extracts.json` or the TitanUp transcripts directly.
