# Titan Content Playbook v2

## Psychology Before Copy

Great captions are rarely won by better adjectives. They are won by
making someone stop scrolling.

Think in this order:

1.  Why would someone stop?
2.  Why would they keep reading?
3.  What value do they get?
4.  What action should they take?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# The 10 Scroll Triggers

## 1. Curiosity

Create an information gap.

Examples - There's one thing every efficient pharmacy has in common... -
We didn't expect this. - The answer surprised us.

Use when: - Customer stories - Product launches - Case studies

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Identity

People engage with content that reinforces who they are.

Examples

-   The best pharmacy owners don't...
-   Modern pharmacies think differently.
-   Owners building for the next decade...

Works because people want to belong to that identity.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Contrarian

Challenge accepted wisdom.

Examples

-   Pharmacy doesn't have a staffing problem.
-   AI isn't replacing pharmacists.
-   More software isn't the answer.

Always back the opinion with evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. Status

People like learning what high performers do differently.

Examples

-   Here's what the highest-performing pharmacies do differently.
-   Every owner we visited had this in common.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Transformation

Show change.

Formula:

Before → Decision → After

Example

Paper everywhere. Workflow rebuilt. 17,000 items a month.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. Narrative

People remember stories more than features.

Simple framework:

Character ↓ Challenge ↓ Decision ↓ Outcome ↓ Lesson

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. Surprise

Reveal something unexpected.

Examples

-   The biggest benefit wasn't speed.
-   The robot wasn't the star.
-   The software wasn't what changed everything.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. Emotion

Even B2B decisions are emotional.

Emotions to target

-   Relief
-   Pride
-   Confidence
-   Ambition
-   Curiosity
-   Belonging
-   Hope

Avoid relying only on excitement.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 9. Visual Thinking

Write captions that create images.

Instead of

"Our workflow is efficient."

Write

"At 4pm every basket is moving. Nobody is shouting. Nobody is looking
for paper."

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 10. Tension

Every good post has friction.

Old way vs new way. Chaos vs calm. Busy vs productive. Technology vs
people. Growth vs stagnation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Writing Principles

## Show, don't tell

Bad

"We're efficient."

Good

"By lunchtime the checking queue had disappeared."

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Be specific

Bad

"We save time."

Good

"We gave pharmacists two hours back every day."

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Use evidence

Numbers. Stories. Customer quotes. Visual descriptions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## End with meaning

Don't simply describe what happened.

Explain why it matters.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Titan Tone

Write like a documentary filmmaker, not a salesperson.

Be: - Curious - Calm - Observational - Evidence-led - Human - Optimistic

Avoid: - Corporate buzzwords - Empty superlatives - Feature dumping -
Forced hype - Generic AI language

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Caption Checklist

Before publishing ask:

-   Does the first line stop the scroll?
-   Is there a story?
-   Is there tension?
-   Is there evidence?
-   Is there a memorable quote?
-   Does it teach something?
-   Could this only have been written about this pharmacy?
-   Does it end with a takeaway instead of a sales pitch?

If seven or eight answers are "yes", it's probably worth posting.
