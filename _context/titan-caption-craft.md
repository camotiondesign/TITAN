# Titan Caption Craft

This file replaces the lost `saas-caption-samples.md`. It is not a rulebook. It is a generation toolkit for hooks, retention, and structure, grounded in captions Titan has actually shipped.

Use it to expand options, not to narrow them. The house rules still apply as a light final pass, and the forbidden-word list and full 13-test self-check live in the voice guide, not here. Everything below is about getting to a strong, hooked, genuinely engaging caption first. Constraint comes last, not first.

**The order of operations:**
0. Read the relevant layer of `titan-voice-canon.md` — the curated, annotated set of the strongest shipped captions. That file is the primary voice source; this file is the toolkit that explains the moves inside it.
1. Name the job (section 1).
2. Win the first 30 words (section 2).
3. Scrub the AI tells (section 3).
4. Pick the structure that fits the asset and the job (section 4).
5. Set the length to the band for that format (section 5).
6. Pull from the swipe file and the canon for a proven shape (section 6).
7. Apply house rules as a final pass, and break them on purpose where it earns it (section 7).
8. Check the series rule and the verbatim rule (section 8).

**v2, July 2026.** Updated after the full dataset merge (Dec 2024 to June 2026, all four platform datasets) and the TitanUp 26 transcripts. Founder voice devices live in `tariq-voice-library.md` (Tariq) and `titan-founder-voices.md` (Wahid, Hooman).

## 1. The job comes first

A caption that hedges two jobs comes out generic. Pick one before writing a word.

| Job | What it optimises for | Typical asset |
|---|---|---|
| **Stop the scroll** | Watch / read-through | Video teasers, vox pops |
| **Build credibility** | Trust, proof | Customer stories, milestones, testimonials |
| **Claim the moment** | Relevance, timeliness | Reactive news, policy, pricing |
| **Own the topic** | Authority, saves | Pillar carousels, frameworks |
| **Start a conversation** | Comments | Pain-point posts, stance, questions |
| **Drive an action** | Clicks, sign-ups | Event reveals, sponsor, sign-ups |

The job decides the structure and the length. A credibility post and a stop-the-scroll post are built differently even off the same footage.

## 2. The first 30 words (the retention engine)

This is the part you want most support on, so it gets the most space. The first line controls everything downstream: the thumb-stop on TikTok, the click-more on LinkedIn, the read past the headline on the blog. Win it or nothing else matters.

### The mechanics (the levers, mix 2-3, not all at once)

These are vehicles, not templates. Vary the sentence shape. A mechanic written as the same construction post after post stops being a technique and becomes a tell.

**Open a loop.** Pose a gap the reader needs closed, then make them watch or read to close it. The loop must be genuine, not "you won't believe what happened next".
- *"How is this pharmacy finishing by 1pm?"* (the answer is the video)

**Lead with the surprising behaviour, not the reaction to it.** Name the strange thing a person did. Withhold why.
- *"A seven-site superintendent who went back to seeing patients."* (running more, but closer to patients, the counterintuitive move)

**Withhold the payoff.** Give the premise, tease the outcome, keep the mechanism in the asset. If the caption prints the punchline, the video has nothing left to earn.
- The trap: the polished profit caption that prints "profit is what makes care possible" hands away the video's best line for free.

**Effect before cause.** Open on the result, not the setup. Cold open on the answer, drop the question that led to it.
- *"Running seven pharmacies could keep anyone stuck in the dispensary."* (effect) then the operator who went the other way (cause)

**Specificity is a hook on its own.** A concrete number or named operator out-pulls any adjective. Vague is the enemy.
- *"What pharmacy owner gets six weeks off?"* beats "how one owner improved his work-life balance"
- Named operator + scale number + transformation is the dominant winning shape for PMR

**Take a stance.** A real position invites a real reply. Soft balance gets scrolled.
- *"We're not in control of the NHS. That's a them problem."*

**Challenge an assumption the reader holds.** A reframe destabilises a belief. It is not a sentence shape, and the vehicle should vary:
- Flat declarative that contradicts the assumption: *"Busy doesn't mean profitable."* (Rahul's own stage line)
- Shift the weight onto the unexpected part: *"The robot isn't the clever part."*
- A question that unsettles the default metric: *"We measure pharmacies by items dispensed. What if that is the wrong number?"*
- Historical parallel: *"In 2006 something happened in dentistry. It is about to happen to pharmacy."* (the dentist analogy)
- Earned redefinition of a loaded word: Tariq arguing profit is what makes care possible. This works because it is a real semantic argument, not a copy trick.

Avoid the reflex *"It is not X, it is actually Y."* It is the most overused reframe construction and reads as machine-written the moment it repeats. Reach for it only when the contrast itself is genuinely the point, and even then, rarely.

**The stakes-first loop (licensed loop variant, added v2.1).** Open loops work only when the first line names the reader's concrete stakes; they fail when curiosity is the only content. *"If you run a pharmacy, you already know the strange part..."* works; *"Does growth feel exciting?"* does not. Evidence: stakes-loop openers lift 1.20 vs baseline; bare question openers 0.76, and on product posts 0.36 — the worst cell in the dataset.

**Conditional licences (added v2.1, from the pattern audit):**
- **Question openers: avoid by default.** Twice condemned by the data. On product posts, banned outright.
- **Cold-punch openers ("Scraps of paper."): high-skill, high-variance.** Median 0.82 across all uses, but the winner on policy posts (1.18/1.20). Licence: the three words must be a vivid image or a genuine gap, never just short. Best home: policy and news.
- **Quote-led openers: home is the customer story.** On stories they win both lenses (1.29 reach / 1.26 interaction); on product posts they suppress reach (0.66). A quote opens a post only when it passes the could-be-any-brand test.

### The first-line tests

Before the second line gets written, the first line must pass:
1. **Stands alone.** It is a complete thought, not "Today we're going to talk about". Setup is not a hook.
2. **Not interchangeable.** Put a competitor's logo on it. Does it still make sense? If yes, it isn't Titan-distinctive yet.
3. **Earns the next line.** A hook sets up a payoff the rest of the caption has to deliver. If line one resolves itself, there is no reason to read line two.
4. **No throat-clearing.** "In the world of pharmacy", "Here at Titan we believe", "As we all know". Cut to the content.

### Three-candidate rule

Never write one first line and run with it. Write three, each using a different mechanic, then pick. The best one is rarely the first one out.

## 3. The AI tells (catch these and kill them)

The fastest way a sharp caption gives itself away as machine-written is sentence shape. The voice guide holds the forbidden-word list. This catches the constructions. For each, the fix is a fresher way to make the same point, not just deletion.

- **The antithesis flip.** "It is not X, it is Y." "This is not about X. It is about Y." The reflex reframe. Handled in section 2: unsettle the assumption through a varied vehicle instead.
- **Rhetorical question then instant answer.** "The result? Huge." "Why does this matter? Because..." "The best part?" A LinkedIn-AI fingerprint. Make the statement directly, or open a loop you do not immediately close.
- **Empty rule-of-three.** "Faster, smarter, simpler." Three abstract parallels carried by rhythm, not meaning. One concrete claim with a number beats three adjectives.
- **SaaS hype adjectives.** Seamless, powerful, robust, effortless, game-changing, revolutionary, cutting-edge. Show the thing instead. "Finishes dispensing by 1pm" beats "powerful time savings".
- **The summarising kicker.** "The bottom line?" "Let that sink in." "And that changes everything." Telling the reader what to feel. Trust the fact to land and end on it.
- **False-drama fragment stacking.** "One platform. One workflow. One source of truth." Fine when each fragment carries distinct meaning, a tell when it is decoration.
- **"Not only X but also Y" and "Less X, more Y".** Symmetry for its own sake. Pick the stronger half and say it plainly.
- **Throat-clearing transitions.** "Now, here is where it gets interesting." "But here is the thing." Just say the interesting thing.
- **The self-caught house tells (found in our own shipped winners, July 2026 canon audit).** Even top performers carried these; they survived despite the tell, not because of it:
  - *"And the best part?"* mid-caption (rhetorical-question-then-answer) — found in the Sajid values post.
  - *"That's not just a feature. That's a different way of working."* (summarising kicker plus antithesis flip stacked) — found in the Rahul notes post. The caption should have ended on the number.
  - *"Seamless integrations"* — banned adjective that slipped into a Titanverse feature post. The integration list needed no adjective.
  - *"That tells you something."* — kicker; only acceptable when the very next line says what it tells you.
  When auditing a draft, check it against these four by name. They are the house's own recurring slips.

The catch-all test: read it aloud. If it sounds like a shape you have seen ten thousand times rather than something a sharp operator or Tariq would actually say, it is a tell. The "he apologised before he said it" line we cut failed exactly this. It was a mouthful no one would say out loud.

## 4. Structures, and when each fits

Structure follows the job and the asset. These are the shapes that have worked, with the right home for each. This list is open. A new structure that beats these is welcome, that is the point of the file.

### The TED two-sentence teaser — for video where the video is the thing
The single most useful video format we have, and the one the old rules would reject.

- **Spec:** two sentences, under 200 characters, payoff withheld. One setup line that names a relatable premise or a surprising behaviour, then a "Here's how / why" handoff, then 👇.
- **When:** any video asset where you want the watch, not the caption. TikTok cuts, keynote clips, vox pops.
- **Why it ignores the dead zone:** the dead zone rule only applies when the caption has to carry the post on its own. A teaser is a doorhanger for the video. Curiosity carries it, so short wins.
- **Watch for:** do not hand over the reframe. Withhold the counterintuitive turn, tease only the obvious bit.
- Shipped: *"Running seven pharmacies could keep anyone stuck in the dispensary. Here's how Steffan John stays with patients and runs a more profitable pharmacy business. 👇"*

### Then → Now → Proof — for customer stories
- **Spec:** where they were (specific pain, specific number), where they are now (specific number, specific change), the named operator quoted as proof.
- **When:** go-live posts, transformation stories, longform customer narratives.
- **Length:** 600-1500 usually, longer if the operator's own voice carries it.
- **Avoid:** Finish → Start → Finish. The Then → Now → Proof arc tests better.
- **Anti-pattern that keeps creeping in:** the feature-list explainer. When the caption turns into a spec sheet of what the system does, it has drifted out of house voice. The quote-led, short-fragment style (Wellesbourne, Stone Age posts) is the anchor. Features belong in the caption bridge or a visual pill grid, never as the body.

### Punchy one-liner — for milestones and events
- **Spec:** one line, one beat, one mark of conviction. Under 100 characters.
- **When:** milestones, brand-defining declaratives, event hype, sign-up drivers.
- Shipped: *"One night in pharmacy you don't want to miss. 24 June. 👉 Register: [link]"*
- Shipped: *"See it run for yourself. Wednesday, Balance Street Pharmacy, Uttoxeter. Link in bio."*
- **Note:** kill the conditional hedge. "If anyone was interested" makes the brand sound apologetic. Direct beats tentative every time.

### Stance longform — for Tariq voice
- **Spec:** stance hook in the first two lines, argument over 3-5 beats, specific evidence, close back on the thesis or a real question.
- **When:** founder-voice posts, policy and contract framing, the reactive news lane in Tariq's register.
- **Length:** 1500+.
- Shipped: the "We're not in control of the NHS / That's a them problem" data post. Note how the stance sits up front and the nuance ("not walking away from the NHS") arrives later, by design. A skimmer sees the stance, a reader gets the nuance.
- **Know going in:** a sharp stance will draw a defending comment. That is the engagement, not a failure. Only soften if the fairness gain beats the punch lost, which it usually does not.

### Analogy-led — for Tariq voice on a single point
- **Spec:** open on one of the six signature analogies (bricklayer, dentist, Tesla, Amazon, boiling frog, skyscraper), land it on a specific pharmacy point.
- **When:** a framework or product truth that an analogy makes instantly legible.
- These are under-used. Each is a post or carousel waiting to happen.

### Testimonial — for a single strong customer line
- **Spec:** the quote does the work. Credibility close ("Thanks for the feedback, [name] ✨"), no link CTA.
- **Length:** ~80-150 characters, can run longer when social-proof density justifies it.
- Shipped: the Tas Bhatti single-image testimonial, ~150 words, justified by owner-plus-sector credibility.

### Quote-led carousel caption — for frameworks
- Slides stay purely informative. The product bridge lives in the caption, never on the slides (media-company test). Cover is a hook of 3-5 words as a proxy for a curiosity gap, breakable when a longer hook earns the swipe.

### The plain announcement — for news the audience already wants (added v2.1)
The most under-rated structure in the dataset. Announcements are the strongest register overall (lift 1.29), and plain ones beat dressed-up ones (1.55 vs 1.24).
- **Spec:** the news, the date/logistics, one beat of conviction or one question. Nothing else. 1-2 techniques maximum; zero is often right.
- **Beats:** [The thing] → [when/where] → [one line of energy or a 👇 question].
- Shipped: *"Tomorrow. / Who's making the trip? 👇 / #TitanUp26"*
- **The rule:** when the news is the value, decoration dilutes. If you catch yourself adding psychology to an announcement, stop.

### The stakes-first mechanism explainer — for reactive policy and finance content (added v2.1)
The BBC explainer shape, translated to captions. Pairs with the ros-atkins-explainer skill.
- **Beats:** stakes (who this hits, second person) → loop opened (the strange part / this might be why) → mechanism unpacked in plain steps → the loss made concrete → circle-of-control turn (what the reader can act on) → product arrives last, honest tense per availability flags.
- **When:** concessions, contract, funding, regulatory mechanics — anywhere the reader's money leaks through a system they don't fully see.
- **Length:** 600-1500. Every stat verified per section 13 before scheduling.

### The cold-punch policy open — for news with a deadline (added v2.1)
- **Spec:** a 2-6 word image or gap, then the date or stakes. Wins both lenses on policy posts (1.18 reach / 1.20 interaction).
- Shipped: *"Hub and spoke. Before the law changes."*
- **Variant for pure distribution:** the stat headline (reach 1.66) — open on the sourced number when you want the post to travel and can accept fewer replies.

### Quote-led customer story — the both-lenses variant (added v2.1)
The only customer-story opening that wins reach AND interaction.
- **Beats:** the operator's verbatim line (distinctive, imperfections kept) → who and where → the honesty beat (the hesitation, the timing that wasn't right) → pressure in loaded fragments → the move they made → specific recent numbers → close on the thesis their story proves, unstated where possible.
- Shipped: the Jaya post (canon PMR #1, top real-engagement post of 2026).
- **Fallback:** direct-statement opening when the goal is pure reach (1.40) and no distinctive quote exists.

### The sponsor problem-first — for partner and exhibitor content (added v2.1)
- **Beats:** the announcement in one line → the reader's pain, repeated concretely ("They phone to... They phone to...") → the partner as resolution → integration line → logistics and 👉.
- Direct-statement opening plus fragment stacking is the winning combination (1.50 interaction; fragments 1.42 vs 1.32). Never open a sponsor post on a number about the sponsor — it suppresses reach (0.61).
- Shipped: the PharmAppy TitanUp post (canon PMR #4).

## 5. Length bands by format (replaces the absolute rule)

The "under 100 or over 600" line is true for feed posts that carry themselves. It is wrong as a blanket rule. The real map:

| Format | Band | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Video teaser** | 100-200 chars | Exempt from the dead zone. Curiosity carries it. |
| **Event / milestone one-liner** | Under 100 chars | One beat, maximum punch. |
| **Testimonial** | ~80-150 chars, flex up on proof density | The quote is the content. |
| **Standalone feed post** | Under 100, or 600-1500 | The dead zone is real here. 100-600 underperforms. |
| **Customer story** | 600-1500 | Room for Then/Now/Proof. |
| **Stance / founder longform** | 1500+ | The longer it runs, the harder the first line must work. |

The dead zone is a property of self-carrying feed posts, not of captions in general. Name the format, then pick the band.

**Evidence status (July 2026 re-check):** on the merged 2026 data, dead-zone posts no longer show a clear ER penalty (PMR medians roughly level; Titanverse dead-zone posts actually edged ahead on a small sample). Treat the dead zone as a **discipline prompt, not a law**: a caption landing at 350 characters should still justify its length deliberately, but "underperforms" is no longer a claim the data supports. Re-validate at the next quarterly canon re-curation; if the pattern holds, retire the zone entirely and keep only the format bands.

## 7. Breaking the rules well (the flexibility licence)

The rules exist to stop weak default output, not to cap strong deliberate output. Break them when you can say why.

- **The dead zone** is broken on purpose for any video teaser. State it: "teaser, dead-zone exempt".
- **The five hook mechanics** in section 2 are a starting set, not a closed list. A new mechanic that beats them is the goal.
- **A new register** (the TED teaser was one) is allowed as a deliberate, labelled experiment. Voice is anchored in the dataset, not frozen to it. When trying a register the dataset does not contain, say so and flag it as a test, do not pretend it is established.
- **The 3-5 word carousel cover** is a proxy for a curiosity gap, not a hard count. A longer cover that earns the swipe wins.

The test for a broken rule is the same as for a kept one: does it serve the job, and does it clear the closest comparable in the swipe file. If yes, ship it.

## 8. The series rule and the verbatim rule (standing, non-negotiable)

**The series rule.** In any series (speaker spotlights, sponsor spotlights, exhibitor interviews, customer story runs), each post is its own shape sourced from its own artefact. The only constant is the closing block (logistics, CTA). The June 2026 Luka exhibitor batch is the cautionary example: four cards shipped with an identical hook-and-kicker template and read as machine output the moment two appeared in the same week. Before drafting post N of a series, reread posts 1 to N-1 and pick a different opening mechanic.

**The verbatim rule (conditional, v2.1).** If a real artefact exists (talk title, transcript line, sponsor claim, operator quote), the verbatim version beats the paraphrase — **in the body and on customer stories.** As an opening line, a quote is licensed only when it passes the could-be-any-brand test and the post is a customer story (where quote-leads win both lenses); on product posts, quote openings suppress reach. A bland quote opening is worse than a number. The canon proves it: nearly every top performer is built around someone's actual words with the imperfections kept. Two hard sub-rules:
- Quotes are single-speaker and traceable to a source (transcript, article, recorded interview). Never composite across speakers — the Wahid/Tariq merge of June 2026 is the standing cautionary example.
- When paraphrasing is unavoidable, say why the verbatim didn't work.

**The fingerprint check.** Before showing any draft, read it against the eight-point fingerprint at the end of `titan-voice-canon.md` (verbatim beats paraphrase, the honesty beat, loaded fragments, numbers as characters, product enters late, British and dry, the close trusts the reader). A draft that ticks the style rules but misses the fingerprint is compliant, not Titan.

## 10. Evidence appendix — the post-type playbook (v2.1, July 2026)

Built from 274 LinkedIn posts (Jun 2025 – Jun 2026), both layers, format-normalised on two lenses: **reach lift** (impressions vs format median) and **interaction lift** (comments ×6 + shares ×4 + reactions, per impression, vs format median — deliberately excludes raw "engagement" counts, which carousel swipe-clicks inflate). Re-run this analysis at every quarterly canon re-curation (`/inc-audit` housekeeping); verdicts below expire if the data moves.

### The two-lens map: every post type has a different job

| Type | n | Reach | Interaction | Job |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Milestone / thanks | 6 | 1.73 | 1.03 | The all-rounder. Rare; use when real. |
| Customer story | 47 | 1.40 | 0.78 | **Reach.** Travels far; low replies is not failure. |
| Policy / news | 32 | 1.14 | 0.94 | **Reach.** |
| Product / feature | 111 | 1.00 | 0.92 | The workhorse; baseline on both lenses. |
| Event hype | 34 | 0.72 | 1.24 | **Resonance.** The room responds; it doesn't travel. |
| Sponsor spotlight | 16 | 0.74 | 1.34 | **Resonance.** |
| Speaker spotlight | 7 | 0.68 | 1.48 | **Resonance.** |
| Cultural moments | 7 | 0.67 | 1.78 | **Resonance.** Highest-resonance register in the dataset. |

Judge a post against its type's job, never against the global average. A mix audit falls out of this table: product-feature is 40% of volume at baseline return; stories and community moments are under-used relative to their lifts.

### Per-type licensed variants

- **Customer story:** quote-led (both lenses: 1.29/1.26) when a distinctive quote exists; direct-statement (reach 1.40) for pure distribution. Structure: quote-led customer story (section 4).
- **Product / feature:** direct-statement default; number-led as the reach variant (1.49/0.83). **Question openers banned in this lane (0.36).**
- **Policy / news:** cold-punch for balance (1.18/1.20); stat headline for distribution (reach 1.66, interaction 0.70). Fragments help most here (1.08 vs 0.85). Structures: cold-punch policy open; stakes-first mechanism explainer.
- **Event hype:** number/date openers (1.29); plain register; countdowns beat cleverness.
- **Sponsor spotlight:** direct-statement + fragments (1.50); never open on a sponsor stat (reach 0.61). Structure: sponsor problem-first.
- **Announcements (cross-type):** strongest register overall (1.29); plain beats dressed (1.55 vs 1.24). Structure: the plain announcement.
- **Cultural moments:** the quiet observational register (canon PMR #7); no product; concrete workplace detail.

### Technique verdicts (format-normalised, whole dataset)

- **Works:** plain announcement register (1.29); stakes-first loop (1.20, n=8); fragment stacking with loaded fragments (1.10, n=100 — the only broadly-proven body technique).
- **Neutral:** link CTAs, second-person density, number openers, stat density, anaphora, ✅ lists, body quotes, question CTAs.
- **Hurts:** bare question openers (0.76; 0.36 on product); cold-punch outside policy (0.82 median, high variance).
- **Density finding:** outside announcements, stacking techniques is flat (0.95/0.93/1.04 across 0-1/2-3/4+). **Job-technique fit beats technique density: 1-2 matched techniques per post, chosen for the type's job.**

### Known limits
Heuristic classifiers; several cells at n=5-8 (quote-led customer stories especially — treat as lead, not law, until more posts accrue); the composite cannot fully separate caption from asset, topic and timing. Pattern-level confidence only. That is why the canon (judgement) and this appendix (measurement) are two files doing two jobs.
