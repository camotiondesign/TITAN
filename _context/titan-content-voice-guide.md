# Titan Content Voice Guide

The voice and craft execution reference for Titan content across all channels. Replaces `linkedin-caption-style-guide.md`. Covers tone, hooks, length, structure, CTAs, voice devices, customer story selection, and the self-check that runs before any draft ships.

**Reading order:** `titan-content-foundations.md` (strategic anchor) → `titan-content-strategy.md` (channel direction) → this file (voice and craft) → channel-specific datasets and source files.

**Status:** v1. Voice principles are evidence-based from LinkedIn dataset analysis, Tariq archive reading, and titanup.club audience signal. Cross-channel voice notes (TikTok, video, blog, email) are strategic plan pending evidence.

---

## 1. The voice principle

> **The Grounded Operator.** Direct, confident, empathetic without being soft, distinctly British. Treats the audience as the smart, busy, sceptical operators they actually are.

The audience writes 600-word feature proposals (Riddhesh) and asks strategic questions about product direction (Mukarrom). They are not the audience for soft corporate warmth. They are the audience for engineering depth, business-model clarity, and stance.

The voice has three layers:

1. **Brand voice (Cam-led)** — the default voice for most Titan content. Sharp, specific, operator-grounded. The willingness-to-adapt operator's voice talking to other willingness-to-adapt operators.
2. **Founder voice (Tariq direct)** — the voice for Tariq-authored content. More polemical, more analogy-driven, more willing to take stances. The same voice that runs in the Pharmacy Business column.
3. **Customer voice (operator-quoted)** — direct quotes from named customers, retained in their phrasing. The most credible voice available; under-used currently.

These layers should be distinguishable. A reader looking at three pieces of Titan content should be able to tell which voice is speaking.

## 3. Style rules (apply across all channels)

### Language

- **British English throughout.** Not Americanisms.
- **No em dashes.** Use commas, full stops, or parentheses.
- **Full stops retained in captions.** Evidence-supported by top-performing post analysis. Do not drop final full stops to look "modern".
- **No "we're delighted to announce" language** unless absolutely necessary (e.g. formal customer announcement that the customer has approved).
- **No bloated LinkedIn waffle.** Cut adjectives that aren't doing work. Cut prepositional phrases that pad without adding meaning. Cut the second sentence if the first one carried the load.

### Emoji set (approved)

✅ 📖 👇 💥 📅 🧠 🎉 ✨

These are the eight approved emojis. Use sparingly — one or two per post maximum unless format clearly warrants more (e.g. a feature comparison carousel slide).

**📍 is not approved.** Flag it whenever it appears and propose a replacement (usually nothing — the emoji is decorative not functional).

### Forbidden words and frames

- **"Proud"** and "we're proud to" — soft corporate. Cam-tested; consistently underperforms.
- **"Exciting", "thrilled", "honoured"** — same category. Soft, performative.
- **"Welcome to"** when describing a customer pharmacy — implies Titan owns or operates the customer site. Use "Meet" or "Visit" or just the customer's name.
- **Exclusivity / scarcity language** — "limited spots", "don't miss out", "exclusive access". Anti-tilt for a pharmacy audience.
- **Vendor framing** — "as your trusted partner", "we work hand-in-hand with". Reads as supplier-of-many, not as a system the operator chose for reasons.
- **Generic "future-of-pharmacy" abstractions** — "the future is here", "pharmacy is changing". True but un-stanceable. Replace with Tariq's specific framings (the dental analogy, the items-to-services thesis).

## 4. Caption length and structure

### Length zones (LinkedIn-evidenced)

**Length doctrine has one home: the format bands in `titan-caption-craft.md` section 5.** This guide no longer states its own length rule; the old absolute "dead zone" rule caused contradictions between files and, on the July 2026 data re-check, is no longer clearly supported by the evidence. The principle that survives here: pick a length band deliberately for the format, and be able to say why the caption is the length it is. Bands, exemptions (video teasers, testimonials, one-liners) and the evidence status all live in the craft file.

### Structure patterns by length

**Under 100 characters:** one line, one beat, one punctuation mark of conviction.
- Example: *"Titan checks every prescription. Every time."*
- Example: *"Pharmacy didn't need another PMR. It needed a new way of working."*

**600-1500 characters:** the **Then → Now → Proof** arc is the preferred structure for customer stories.
- Then: where the customer was before (operational pain, specific items number)
- Now: where the customer is today (specific items number, specific change)
- Proof: the named operator quoted, the specific transformation
- Avoid the Finish → Start → Finish structure; the Then → Now → Proof arc tests better

**1500+ characters:** the full essay structure. Hook in the first 2 lines. Argument developed over 3-5 beats. Specific evidence (named operator, specific number, quoted line). A closing stance or question.

## 5. Hook patterns (the first line is everything)

The first line is the hook. On LinkedIn it controls click-through; on TikTok it controls thumb-stop; on blog and email it controls whether the reader continues past the headline. In all cases the first line is the single highest-leverage element of the entire piece.

### Five hook patterns (evidence-based)

**Note (July 2026):** hooks are an open toolkit, not a closed list. The generative mechanics (open loop, surprising behaviour, withhold the payoff, effect-before-cause, specificity, stance, assumption-challenge) live in `titan-caption-craft.md` section 2, which is the working reference when drafting. The five patterns below stay as the evidence-based reference set.

**Pattern 1 — Punchy short statement.** Milestones, transformation, brand-defining moments.
- *"Titan checks every prescription. Every time."*
- *"10 million items in a single month."*
- *"Pharmacy didn't need another PMR. It needed a new way of working."*

**Pattern 2 — Reframe / "rules have changed".** Policy, contract, regulatory, pricing, category-shift moments.
- *"What's Changing in the Pharmacy Contract — And When."*
- *"Bulk nomination is undermining patient choice."*
- *"Some pharmacies might walk away from Pharmacy First. And it's not hard to see why."*

**Pattern 3 — Self-aware question.** Pain-point engagement, workflow critique.
- *"Does growth feel exciting? Or just overwhelming?"*
- *"Your Pharmacy Isn't Struggling. Your PMR Is."*

**Pattern 4 — Statistic-led.** Confirmed-claim news reactives only. Never made-up; always traceable.
- *"73% of people in pharmacy said expanding private services is their top priority this year."*
- *"Nearly half of pharmacies haven't submitted their Data Security Toolkit."*

**Pattern 5 — Analogy-led** (for Tariq-voiced content). Apply one of the six signature analogies to a specific pharmacy point.
- *"If I pay a bricklayer a fee per brick to supply and fit..."* (bricklayer)
- *"In 2006 something significant happened in the dental profession."* (dentist)
- *"It was the same expression I had when I first pressed auto-steer on a Tesla."* (Tesla)

### Hooks to avoid

- **Soft openings.** *"In the world of pharmacy..."* / *"Here at Titan we believe..."* / *"As we all know..."*
- **Generic questions that anyone could ask.** *"Are you struggling with stock?"* (no — too generic). *"Stock issues bleed 41% of margin. What's your biggest leak?"* (yes — specific signal + question).
- **Vague intriguing-without-content.** *"There's something every pharmacy owner needs to know."* Reads as clickbait.
- **The "let me tell you about..." setup.** Drop the throat-clearing; lead with the content.

### The first-line test

Before any caption ships:
1. Does the first line stand alone as a complete thought? (Not "Today we're going to talk about..." — that's setup, not hook.)
2. Could this first line appear under any competitor's logo and make sense? (If yes, it's not Titan-distinctive.)
3. Does it commit to a frame the rest of the caption has to deliver on? (Hooks should set up a payoff.)

## 6. CTA patterns

### Question CTAs (👇)

Default for engagement posts. The closing CTA is a specific question the audience can answer in the comments.

- *"What's the time thief in your pharmacy? 👇"*
- *"Which of these is the biggest barrier in your business? 👇"*
- *"What would you add to this list? 👇"*

**Rule:** the question must be specific and answerable. Generic questions ("What do you think? 👇") get generic answers (silence). Audience-validated questions (tied to titanup.club poll signal) get audience engagement.

### Link CTAs (👉)

Reserved for sponsor and event reveal posts only. **Not used for general engagement posts.** The link CTA signals "click here for the thing" rather than "talk to us in the comments".

- *"RSVP at titanup.club 👉"*
- *"Read more in the latest issue of Pharmacy Business 👉"*
- *"Meet our sponsor [X] at TitanUp 26 👉"*

### Owned-destination CTAs (special category)

Posts deliberately routing rented audience to owned audience get a clear destination CTA — typically titanup.club or a future email signup. These are not generic link CTAs; they are subscriber-acquisition CTAs.

- *"Join the conversation at titanup.club ✨"*
- *"Get the framework as a PDF — link in comments"*
- *"Subscribe to The Operating Stack newsletter, monthly from Tariq Muhammad."*

These CTAs are concentrated on Wednesdays and Thursdays (the LinkedIn cadence days designated for owned-destination routing).

### CTAs to avoid

- **No CTA at all.** Every post should have one explicit ask. The "let people decide what to do" approach is anti-Content-Inc.
- **Two CTAs in one post.** Pick one. Either comment OR click OR subscribe. Not all three.
- **Soft asks.** "Let us know your thoughts" / "Drop a like if you agree". These are weak signals that don't drive measurable behaviour.

## 8. Customer story selection lens

Every customer story should make the **willingness-to-adapt** visible. The protagonist chose to change. They didn't have change happen to them; they ran toward the tool.

### Selection criteria

A strong Titan customer story has:

1. **Named operator.** Real first name, real pharmacy name. Anonymous stories underperform.
2. **Specific scale number.** Items per month before, items per month after. Or a specific transformation metric (services revenue, hours saved per pharmacist per week, retention of patients). Non-specific stories underperform.
3. **Before/after transformation arc.** The Then → Now → Proof structure. Not "we're going well" but "we were here, now we're here".
4. **The willingness-to-adapt visible.** The operator chose to change something. Cite the specific choice — replaced legacy PMR, restructured the team, killed the paper workflow.
5. **A quotable line.** The operator in their own words saying something memorable. "I get to see my kids at weekends" / "I can sleep at night now" / "We've doubled items without doubling staff" — operator-tested, not marketing-written.

### Customer story formats and where each one goes

| Format | Length | Primary channel | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Longform video | 4-7 minutes | YouTube + LinkedIn | The canonical format. Drives engagement on LinkedIn and serves as the durable YouTube asset. |
| Short video clip | 30-60 seconds | TikTok + LinkedIn (caveat) | TikTok works for these. LinkedIn evidence shows short clips reach but don't drive comments — use selectively. |
| Carousel | 6-10 slides | LinkedIn | Hits the Cluster 1 winning pattern when structured as Then → Now → Proof. |
| Single-image with quote | 1 image | LinkedIn | Effective for the punchy quote moment with named-operator credibility. |
| Written case study | 800-1500 words | titanpmr.com / titanverse.com | The high-intent conversion asset with PDF gate. |
| Email feature | 200-400 words | The monthly newsletter | The "customer story of the month" newsletter section. |
| Blog longform | 1500-3000 words | titanpmr.com | The durable text version, optimised for SEO and linkable from elsewhere. |
| Live operator on stage | 15-20 minutes | TitanUp | The highest-credibility version — the operator telling their story live. |

A single customer (Sagar, Geoff, Brighton Hill, BMP, etc.) can and should be told in multiple formats across multiple channels. The longform film is the source asset; the other formats are channel-native cuts.

### Customer story anti-patterns

- **Generic "they love Titan" stories.** No transformation arc, no specific number, no named choice — not a story.
- **Feature-led stories disguised as customer stories.** ("This customer uses Batch Flow and finds it helpful." — not a story.)
- **The operator's name buried at the end.** The named operator is the hook; lead with them.
- **Three customers in one post.** Pick one. The audience commits to one specific human transformation; three blurs the focus.
- **Quotes that read like marketing copy.** "Titan has transformed our pharmacy" — too polished. Quote the operator's actual phrasing even when it's slightly imperfect.

## 9. Polarising stance content

Tariq takes stances in print that don't appear on LinkedIn currently. The brand voice tends to dilute the stance below the threshold where it generates conversation.

### When to take a stance

- **A regulatory shift is being discussed.** Contract changes, IP wave, NHSBSA policy. The audience is forming opinions; the brand can either lead the conversation or follow it.
- **A specific competitor or industry actor is failing publicly.** LloydsPharmacy closing, multiples retreating, trade bodies blanketly recommending "turn off the lights" protests.
- **A common cope needs naming.** "Hoping the NHS contract will improve" / "Waiting for the funding to come back" — Tariq names these directly in print.

### How to take a stance

Three approaches in increasing intensity:

**Approach 1 — Quote Tariq.** The brand carries the stance with full attribution. *"As Tariq Muhammad has written in Pharmacy Business: 'The NHS Pharmacy contract is fundamentally flawed and the government has no money.'"* This is the most defensible approach. Tariq carries the heat; the brand carries the platform.

**Approach 2 — Take an aligned stance in brand voice.** The brand voice takes a position consistent with Tariq's print stance but in its own words. Less sharp than Tariq's voice but braver than no stance at all. *"The contract isn't going to save pharmacy. Pharmacies who think it might are running out of time."*

**Approach 3 — Stand on the founder's stance directly.** Tariq posts the polarising line on his own LinkedIn profile; the Titan brand reshares. This is the highest-intensity version and uses the founder-led play.

### Stance content anti-patterns

- **Hedging the stance until it's not a stance.** "The contract is challenging for many" — true but un-stanceable. Either say what's wrong with the contract or don't pretend to have an opinion.
- **Stance without evidence.** Polarising opinions need to be defensible. Tariq's stances are defensible because they're backed by 25 years of context.
- **Stances on topics outside the unified tilt.** Don't take stances on UK politics, NHS-wide policy unrelated to pharmacy, or industry topics that aren't connected to the items-to-services thesis. The stance is a lever, not a free-for-all.

## 10. Fact discipline

All statistics in published content must be traceable to primary sources.

### Primary sources

- **CPE** (cpe.org.uk)
- **GOV.UK / NHSBSA**
- **Pharmaceutical Journal**
- **Chemist+Druggist**
- **Official NHS England statements**
- **Tariq's published articles** (for Titan-specific stats — these are first-party and citable)
- **`titan-audience-voice.md`** (for titanup.club poll results — these are first-party Titan audience signal)

### Categorisation

For each factual claim in a draft:

- **Confirmed** — backed by a primary source. Link the source.
- **Needs tightening** — partially accurate but framed in a way that overstates or misstates. Rewrite for accuracy before publishing.
- **Unverifiable** — cannot locate a primary source. Either cut the claim or rewrite to avoid making a specific factual assertion.

Anything in "needs tightening" or "unverifiable" must be fixed before scheduling. The reactive news lane is one of the strongest-performing content pillars but loses credibility fast if claims are sloppy.

### Constructed links

Constructed links between separate studies must be made explicit. If Study A says X and Study B says Y, and the post implies X-because-of-Y, the post must make the inference visible ("These two trends together suggest...") rather than asserting the link as established fact.

## 11. The self-check before publish (13 tests)

Every draft passes through this checklist before shipping. From `/captions` Phase C, restated here as a standalone reference.

1. **Hook test** — real hook (matches one of the five patterns in Section 5) or soft opener?
2. **Soft-version test** — does the draft contain the predictable language Claude would have written by default? (Name the soft version before drafting; commit to not writing it.)
3. **Job test** — doing the singular job for the post, not hedging two jobs?
4. **Stack-framework test** — is the post locatable in the four-layer stack? Does it connect to adjacent layers where natural?
5. **Unified tilt test** — does the caption reinforce one or more elements of the unified tilt (AI-powered / operating stack / pharmacy entrepreneurs / items-to-services / margin and team time)?
6. **Texture test** — sounds like the brand voice (or founder voice, or customer voice) and not B2B SaaS approximation?
7. **Distinctiveness test** — Titan-specific or could-be-any-pharmacy-SaaS? (The logos-stripped test from Pulizzi's tilt lens.)
8. **Voice device check** — if a Tariq analogy fits the topic, is it used? If a polarising stance is appropriate, is the stance sharp enough?
9. **Style guide rule pass** — no em dashes, no proud-language, British English, approved emoji set only, CTA pattern correct?
10. **Factual accuracy** — does the caption claim or imply anything the knowledge file doesn't support? Are stats from approved primary sources?
11. **Best-in-dataset** — draft clears the 2-3 closest comparable posts from the LinkedIn dataset?
12. **Variety test** — same hook pattern not repeated across multiple posts in the batch?
13. **Ship test** — could this publish today without rewriting?

A draft failing any test goes back for rework. Cam shouldn't ship below this bar; Claude shouldn't propose below this bar.

## 13. When this guide and the data disagree

The data wins on what's been tested. The guide holds on what hasn't yet been.

**Example:** the guide says "use Then → Now → Proof for customer stories" because that's tested in the LinkedIn data. If a future dataset refresh shows another structure performs better, the data wins and this guide gets updated.

**Example:** the guide says "approved emoji set is ✅ 📖 👇 💥 📅 🧠 🎉 ✨" because that's Cam's tested set. If a dataset analysis surfaces another emoji that consistently outperforms, the data wins.

**But:** when there's no dataset to test against (TikTok, blog, email at launch), the guide is the operating reference. The strategic plan holds until evidence accumulates.

The voice guide is durable but not immutable. Update it when evidence forces an update; hold it when evidence supports it.
